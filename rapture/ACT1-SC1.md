# RAPTURE — ACT ONE, SCENE 1

**INT./EXT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE — MORNING (PRESENT DAY)**

A big shabby converted house. Twenty-odd residents. Breakfast chaos.

**STYLE / MOVEMENT NOTE (verbatim):** this scene is the **only** place in Nina's thread that moves. Handheld, busy, following her through the building — because the rapture takes all of it away and the rest of her season is static and wide. The stillness later means nothing unless we've seen this. Ideally built as two or three long takes; the shots below are beats within them.

**LOOK:** same stylized DNA as the cold open, translated to morning — heightened contrast, muted desaturated palette, soft halation on practicals, fine grain, gentle vignette; cold grey British morning daylight through steamed windows, weak sun-bars, shabby warm interiors, steam and toast-smoke, tungsten glow against grey light.

**HOUSE GEOMETRY (locked):** pebbledash Victorian facade with hand-painted "ST JUDE'S" sign; front door into a long shabby hallway (crooked frames, coats on hooks, noticeboard, worn carpet runner); kitchen off the hall (yellowed tiles, small radio on a shelf); worn carpeted staircase with chipped bannister; cramped office (filing cabinet + ancient fax); utility room; long dining room with one long table.

---

## CAST (locked descriptions — repeated verbatim in every generation prompt)

**NINA (45):** dark hair in a low practical bun with grey streaks at the temples, strong plain face, no makeup, tired warm brown eyes. Faded olive-green work cardigan over a blue checked shirt, sleeves pushed to the elbows, a caretaker's keys on a strap at her waist, sensible trousers and shoes. Not warm, not unkind. Entirely occupied.

**COL (mid-30s):** shaved head, neat short beard, tidy pressed grey polo shirt and clean track bottoms. Holds a mug carefully with both hands. Calm, fragile, pleased with himself.

**VOLUNTEER (early 20s):** open keen face, bright orange lanyard, beige puffer gilet over a hoodie. Going to be gone in nine minutes and nobody will ever thank him.

**RESIDENT (60s, utility):** grey cardigan, thinning hair, lived-in face; one of the long-termers.

**YOUNG WOMAN (mid-20s, dining):** quiet, dark hoodie, hair in a loose knot, eyes that haven't slept.

**BACKGROUND RESIDENTS:** mixed ages, shabby warm clothes — jumpers, dressing gowns, beanies. The house has twenty-odd of them and it should feel like it.

## CONTINUITY NOTES

- **The keys on the strap** are Nina's spine — visible at the sink (2, 3), the hall (4, 9), the stairs (5). They never leave her waist until the season's stillness does.
- **Col's mug** (6) reappears in 21: upright, full, on the third step. He never finished it.
- **The ledger "22"** (8) is the truth against the phone lie "fourteen" (7). Same neat hand, same blue ink.
- **The tap** (12) is the season's clock — it starts here, unnoticed, and the house's failures accumulate from it.
- **The crate** (9) lands in the utility (11) and stays where she dumped it (21).
- **The rapture grammar is identical to the cold open:** no flash, no sound, no wind; the camera hunts for people who have left frame; a rocking cup and a spinning knife are the only punctuation.
- **Movement grammar:** everything through shot 21 is handheld and alive; 21 is the last handheld in her thread — the camera visibly settles and stops; 22 begins the locked-off, wide aftermath language.
