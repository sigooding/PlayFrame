# Scene 21A — DRAFT for director approval (not in the Fountain)

Insert between scene 21 (Jack's office, Vera's coffee, "Tomorrow, then.") and scene 22 (the noodle counter, 11:40 p.m.).
Purpose: plant the Vera–Jack relationship before 22. Friction, competence, one small kindness, and Jack's secret underneath.
No existing scene changes. Rain continues from 21; scene 22 is that night.

---

EXT. KANDA ARCADE, SHUTTERED STALLS - DAY #21A#

A covered shopping arcade, half the shutters down. Rain drums on the corrugated roof. Neon dribbles across wet tile.

Vera walks fast, a paper list in her hand. Jack keeps pace without seeming to hurry. He holds a folded umbrella he hasn't opened.

VERA
Third one today.

JACK
Fourth. You skipped the tobacconist.

VERA
Because he's rude.

JACK
Which is why he knows things.

She stops at a narrow shopfront: pencils in jars, pads of paper, a hand-lettered sign. A ceramic cat on the step with a chipped saucer in front of it.

INT. ART SUPPLY SHOP, KANDA ARCADE - CONTINUOUS

A shop the width of a hallway. Charcoal, ink, cheap watercolour boxes. An OLD SHOPKEEPER sits behind the counter peeling a mandarin.

Jack bows slightly. Picks up a stick of charcoal. Puts coins on the counter without being asked, and says something warm and unimportant about the rain.

JACK
(in Japanese)
Terrible weather for business.

SHOPKEEPER
(in Japanese)
Good for charcoal. Nobody goes out. Everybody draws.

Vera hovers by the door, understanding one word in four. Jack buys a second stick he doesn't need. The shopkeeper relaxes by degrees.

JACK
(in Japanese)
A friend of ours. Foreign girl. Short hair, red clip. She buys here.

The shopkeeper stops peeling.

SHOPKEEPER
(in Japanese)
The one who talks to the cat.

Vera hears "cat" and steps forward.

VERA
What did he say?

JACK
She was a regular. Paid in small coins. Never in a hurry.

The shopkeeper says something longer, with a shrug. Jack listens. His face does not change.

JACK (CONT'D)
He says she was lonely.

VERA
(flat)
That's not what he said.

A beat. Jack holds her look.

VERA (CONT'D)
I don't have much Japanese. I have excellent ears. He said a lot more than that.

JACK
He said she was a nice girl and she was running out of money. And that she looked like someone waiting for a call.

The shopkeeper nods, as though to say that is exactly it.

VERA
Thank you.

JACK
For what?

VERA
For saying it.

Jack doesn't answer. He turns back to the shopkeeper and, from now on, translates every word.

The shopkeeper pushes a small dish across the counter: coins.

SHOPKEEPER
(in Japanese)
Hers. Always left her change. I keep it. For the cat.

Vera crouches at the door, to the chipped saucer on the step. Among the yen: one American quarter, worn smooth.

She picks it up. Turns it over. It is the coin her mother gave each of them, once, in a different life.

VERA
She still has one.

Jack, behind her, says nothing. He has seen a coin like it in a box in his cupboard.

EXT. KANDA ARCADE, SHUTTERED STALLS - LATER

They come out. The rain has thickened. Vera has her list and her bag and a roll of paper the shopkeeper pushed on her, and nowhere to put anything.

Jack unfolds the umbrella and holds it out, not quite over her. She looks at it. At him. At the rain.

VERA
You don't have to.

JACK
I know.

She ducks under. Shoulders almost touching, neither looking at the other. He takes the roll of paper without being asked and tucks it under his arm, on the wet side.

They walk. She notices the wet shoulder. She says nothing, and moves a little closer so the umbrella covers it.

VERA
You look at people, not rooms. I look at rooms.

JACK
Then between us.

He stops. He didn't mean to say it. She doesn't pick it up. She is quietly, privately pleased, the way people are when they've decided not to be.

A bright sign flickers over the street. She says, without looking at him:

VERA
I'm glad it's you.

Jack keeps walking. The umbrella does not waver, and his face does.
