Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

EXT. RAILWAY MAINTENANCE WALKWAY - CONTINUOUS #91#

A narrow steel walkway beside the tracks. Behind them, a door bangs open on the Hive's roof. Flashlight beams sweep the aerials and water tanks.

And ahead, the rails begin to sing. A light grows at the far end of the viaduct.

A train.

Jack pulls Vera flat against the fence at the edge of the walkway. There is nowhere else to go.

The train arrives. A roaring wall of noise and wind and lit windows, a metre from their faces, strobing across them. Passengers inside reading, sleeping, looking at their phones. None of them see.

Vera and Jack are pressed together against the fence, faces inches apart, rain and train-light washing over them.

She doesn't pull away this time.

On the Hive's roof, the flashlight beams search the tracks. The train stands between them and the walkway, carriage after carriage.

When the last carriage passes, the walkway is empty.
