INT. VERA'S APARTMENT - CONTINUOUS #4#

Small and tidy. Grey light through rain-streaked glass. A smartphone on the table; an old boxy television in the corner. Nothing here quite belongs to one decade.

By the door, in the umbrella stand: a pale blue umbrella. Bone dry.

On a shelf, one framed photograph, its colors gone warm and faded. A Tokyo street, twenty years ago. An American man in a rumpled suit, smiling, holding the hands of two small girls: VERA (9), serious, and MARA (4), mid-laugh. Behind them, the sign of a noodle shop.

VERA VOSS (29) sits at the low table. Two cups. Hers holds cold tea. The other is empty and clean, set out as though someone is expected.

Her phone lies face up in front of her. She calls. Waits.

MARA (V.O.)
(recorded; bright)
It's Mara. You know what to do.

BEEP.

Vera opens her mouth. For a moment, nothing.

VERA
It's me again.
(beat)
I'm not angry anymore. I wasn't really angry then, either. I just --
(stops herself)
You left your umbrella. You'll get soaked.
(beat)
Just call me. You don't have to say anything. Just call, so I know.

She hangs up.

The old television murmurs to itself.

WEATHER ANNOUNCER (ON TV)
(in Japanese)
...rain continuing through the rest of the week...

She looks at the umbrella by the door. At the clean, empty cup. At the photograph.

She stands. Puts on her coat. Hesitates at the door.

Then she takes the blue umbrella. Mara's umbrella.
