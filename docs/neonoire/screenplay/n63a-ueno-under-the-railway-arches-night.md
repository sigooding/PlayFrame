EXT. UENO, UNDER THE RAILWAY ARCHES - NIGHT #63A#

A row of brick arches with shops in them: shuttered, lit, shuttered. Rain running down the ironwork. Jack comes out of the locker room with his coat buttoned over the box and something flat inside it.

At the far end of the arch, a black sedan sits with its lights off and its engine running.

Jack doesn't look at it. He walks.

Behind him, the sedan's lights come on. It follows at walking pace.

He turns into a doorway of noise: a PACHINKO PARLOUR, a wall of steel balls and light and a hundred people staring at their machines. He walks straight through, between the rows, out through the fire door at the back and into an alley of dripping air conditioners.

He waits. Watches the far end of the alley.

Two men in dark coats run past the mouth of it, scanning, and are gone. Behind them, in the street, the sedan crawls along the kerb and stops. Someone inside is speaking into a sleeve. We can't hear it. We can see him give up.

A last train pulls out overhead. The arches shake.

Jack takes the cassette from inside his coat and looks at it, in the light of a vending machine.

He can't carry it any more. Not another day.
