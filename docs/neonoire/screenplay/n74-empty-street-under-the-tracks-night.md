EXT. EMPTY STREET UNDER THE TRACKS - NIGHT
Source: the final screenplay (Neonoire (3).fountain, September 2026), reproduced verbatim below its own heading.
Cast: Vera Voss, Jack Voss.
Grammar: The film's biggest street frame: a row of vending machines as the only light, the black road shining like water, the elevated railway crossing 
overhead, two small figures twenty metres apart. The violence is one desperate blow, not a scene; the breaking is a folding, not a scream. The 
reflection may carry a figure under a pale-blue umbrella and must never resolve. Then WIDE, then EXTREME WIDE, and the score enters only when the shot 
has held too long.

EXT. EMPTY STREET UNDER THE TRACKS - NIGHT #74#

A long, empty street beside the elevated railway. Shuttered shops. A row of vending machines, their cold white light the only light, rain falling through it in columns. The black road shining like water.

In a city of fourteen million people, no one else is here.

Jack walks out of the dark from the direction of the Hive. His coat is soaked. His hands are dark. He has not washed them.

He sees her at the far end of the street.

He stops under the awning of a closed shop.

Vera slows. Stops. Twenty metres apart.

For a moment neither of them says anything. She is crying, almost silently.

Jack takes one step toward her.

She shakes her head.

VERA
Don't.

He stops.

She looks at him. There is something almost childlike in her face now. Not rage. Disbelief. As though she is still hoping he will tell her that what she's heard is wrong.

She walks toward him. Slowly. One shoe, one bare foot on the wet road.

VERA (CONT'D)
You knew.

Jack doesn't answer.

Her face changes. That silence is the answer.

She hits him. Not a big cinematic slap. One desperate blow against his chest with her fist.

He takes it.

She hits him again. Then again.

VERA (CONT'D)
You knew where she was.

He says nothing. He can't. Behind his silence is a promise to a dead girl, and the audience knows it, and Vera doesn't.

She hits him again, weaker. And then the anger drains out of her all at once. Her hands just fall.

VERA (CONT'D)
(whispering)
Why didn't you tell me?

Jack opens his mouth. Nothing comes.

VERA (CONT'D)
She was waiting for me.

That line is almost inaudible.

Jack looks away.

VERA (CONT'D)
And I was waiting for you.
(beat)
And you were with her.

Jack closes his eyes.

And that is when Vera finally breaks. Not screaming. She simply folds. Her knees hit the wet pavement.

Jack drops down instinctively, reaching for her.

Her hands grab the front of his coat. She holds on. For one second it looks as if she is going to let him hold her.

Then she realises whose coat it is.

She pushes him away.

And sits there, in the rain, in the red dress.

Jack kneels a few feet away. He doesn't touch her again.

WIDE. EXTREME WIDE.

Two small figures on a huge, empty, shining street. The vending machines hum. The rain falls through their light.

Every sound drops away except the rain.

In the black water beside Vera, her reflection. And in the reflection, for less than a second, the street is slightly different: a shutter open that is closed, a warm light in a window that is dark, and a figure under a pale blue umbrella who could be Mara, or could be no one.

It is gone.

Vera covers her face with her hands.

Above them, an elevated train passes, a long ribbon of lit windows. Inside, strangers sleep, read, stare at their phones. Nobody looks down.

Only now does the SCORE enter: sparse and mournful, as if coming from very far away.

The shot holds. Long enough that it becomes hard to watch. There is nothing Jack can say. Nothing that fixes it.
