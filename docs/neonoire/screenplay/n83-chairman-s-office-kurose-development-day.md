Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

INT. CHAIRMAN'S OFFICE, KUROSE DEVELOPMENT - DAY #83#

The fortieth floor. Rain on the glass. Tokyo grey and endless below.

Vera sits in a leather chair in front of Kurose's desk. She wears a plain dark coat. Her father's notebook is on her lap, her hands flat on its cover.

Kurose sits across from her. Courteous. Curious. He pours her tea himself.

KUROSE
When my secretary said the daughter of Daniel Voss wanted to see me, I didn't believe her.

His English is perfect. Old-fashioned.

KUROSE (CONT'D)
You have his eyes. He looked at people the same way. As if he expected them to tell the truth.

VERA
Did you?

KUROSE
To him? I don't remember.
(beat)
Your father was a good man, Miss Voss. I mean that sincerely. There are not many.
(beat)
Good men are very expensive.

Vera doesn't touch the tea.

KUROSE (CONT'D)
What is it you want? Money is vulgar. But it is also useful. For a young woman alone, in a foreign city.

VERA
I wanted to see your face.

KUROSE
And?

VERA
It's just a face.

For the first time, something flickers in him. Not fear. Irritation, perhaps, at being looked at like that.

Vera stands. She holds up the notebook. Not threatening. Just showing it to him.

VERA (CONT'D)
My father kept notes. He wrote your name on every page.
(beat)
And now I know.

She walks to the door. Stops beside the glass case with the model of the redevelopment inside. She looks down at it: the little white towers, the little plaza, the fountain where the Hive is.

VERA (CONT'D)
It's very clean.

She goes.

Kurose sits still for a moment. Then he presses a button on his desk phone.

KUROSE
(in Japanese; into phone)
Follow her.
