INT. POLICE STATION, INTERVIEW ROOM - MOMENTS LATER #6#

Small. A table, two chairs, a box of tissues nobody has touched in years. Rain on a frosted window.

DETECTIVE ISHIDA (50s) sets a paper cup of tea in front of Vera. Gentle. Unhurried. Tired, in a way that looks like decency.

ISHIDA
You must be cold.

His English is excellent.

VERA
(in Japanese)
I speak Japanese.

ISHIDA
Of course. But this is a difficult thing. It is easier in your own language.

A small beat. She lets it go.

VERA
Is something wrong? He looked at the screen, and then --

ISHIDA
Nothing is wrong. We just like to take care with these things.
(sits)
Voss. You grew up here?

VERA
Until I was nine.

ISHIDA
It must have changed very much.

VERA
Not as much as I thought.

He smiles at that, as if it pleases him.

ISHIDA
When did you last see your sister?

VERA
Three days ago. At my apartment. We had dinner.

ISHIDA
And how did she seem?

Vera hesitates.

VERA
Normal.

Ishida waits, kindly, as if he knows there is more. She doesn't give it.

ISHIDA
Did she ever mention Kanda? Friends there? Work?

VERA
Kanda? No. Why Kanda?

ISHIDA
We ask about many places.

VERA
You asked about one.

A small pause. Ishida smiles mildly, as if she has caught him in something harmless.

ISHIDA
You would make a good detective.
(gently)
Go home, Miss Voss. Keep your phone on. If she calls, call me first. Any hour.

He slides his card across the table. She takes it. Stands. Picks up the blue umbrella.

VERA
She will call.

ISHIDA
I'm sure she will.

She goes. The door closes behind her.

Ishida sits alone a moment, looking at the untouched tea.
