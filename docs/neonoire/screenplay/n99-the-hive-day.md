Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

EXT. THE HIVE - DAY #99#

Weeks later.

The Hive is coming down.

Excavators bite into the walls with long steel jaws. Concrete falls in slow grey curtains. Dust hangs in the air.

The building has been cut open like the illustrated cross-sections in an old book. Rooms stand exposed to the sky, stacked one above another: a wall of flowered wallpaper, a calendar still pinned beside a window, a mirror, a child's height marks pencilled on a doorframe.

The camera tracks slowly sideways along the ruined face of the building, as it tracked along the lit windows of the living one. Empty room after empty room. We remember who lived in each.

At the barrier on the street, a small crowd watches. The radio repairman. The family. The girl with the violin case.

Kaneko stands among them, holding the old noodle-shop sign against her chest, wrapped in a blanket.
