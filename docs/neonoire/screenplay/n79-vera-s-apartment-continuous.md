Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

INT. VERA'S APARTMENT - CONTINUOUS #79#

Vera sits at the low table in the dawn light. Beside her, the two cups from the beginning of the film, both empty now. She turns the pages.

Her father's handwriting. Neat, slanted, careful.

DANIEL (V.O.)
Shiohama. Third visit. The residents say men came in the spring with flowers, and then in the summer with iron bars. The fire began in two places at once. Fires don't do that.

She turns pages. The handwriting grows faster.

DANIEL (V.O.) (CONT'D)
The claim will be paid. Everyone wants it paid. The company, the police, the man who owns the land now. Kurose. Always Kurose.

Pages.

DANIEL (V.O.) (CONT'D)
The police have given me a young detective who speaks English. They call him Jack. He's bright. He believes me. We ate noodles at Kaneko's and I told him everything and he wrote it all down.

Vera stops. Reads that line again.

Her hand goes to her mouth.

She turns the page. The line we didn't see Jack read, underlined twice.

DANIEL (V.O.) (CONT'D)
Today Jack signed the report. Accidental fire. I don't blame him. He is twenty-eight years old and they own his whole life. But I thought, of all of them, he would stand with me.

Vera stares at the page.

DANIEL (V.O.) (CONT'D)
I'll go on alone. I've done it before.

The last pages. The handwriting is almost unrecognisable now.

DANIEL (V.O.) (CONT'D)
They followed me home again tonight. The girls were asleep. I stood at the door a long time.
(beat)
If something happens to me, it will not be what they say. Tell Vera. When she is old enough. Tell her it will not be what they say.

Vera closes the notebook.

For twenty years she believed her father chose to leave her.

She sits in the grey light, holding the notebook against her chest, the way she held the lighter.

She has been given back her father and taken Jack away in the same hour. She doesn't know which to feel first.
