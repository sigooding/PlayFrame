Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

INT. VERA'S APARTMENT, BATHROOM - CONTINUOUS #53#

A small tiled bathroom. Jack sits on the closed toilet. Vera kneels in front of him with a bowl of warm water and a cloth, cleaning the cut at his hairline.

He watches her face while she works. Very closely. As though he is trying to read something written there.

VERA
Who did this?

JACK
I don't know.

VERA
You don't know?

JACK
Eight men. Masks. They didn't introduce themselves.

She wrings out the cloth. Pink water.

VERA
You told me it was just an inn.

JACK
It was just an inn.

She looks up and catches him watching her.

VERA
What?

He doesn't answer. He's still watching. Measuring. She sees something in his look that she doesn't understand, and it frightens her more than the blood.

VERA (CONT'D)
Jack. What?

He looks at her hands: careful, steady, stained pink. At her face: exhausted, afraid for him.

Whatever he was looking for, it isn't there. It was never there.

JACK
Nothing.
(beat)
I'm sorry. Nothing.

She puts a plaster on the cut, very gently. Her hand stays on his face a moment after.

He covers her hand with his.

And then, without either of them deciding, she kisses him. Or he kisses her. It doesn't matter which.

It is quiet, and brief, and it changes everything.

When it ends, she rests her forehead against his.

VERA
Don't go anywhere else without telling me.

JACK
I won't.

It is another promise. He is collecting them.
