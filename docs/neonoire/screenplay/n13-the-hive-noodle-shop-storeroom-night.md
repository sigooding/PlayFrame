INT. THE HIVE, NOODLE SHOP STOREROOM - NIGHT
Source: the final screenplay (Neonoire (3).fountain, September 2026), reproduced verbatim below its own heading. Written, not boarded: the numbered shot board covers the opening scenes only.
Cast: — (not boarded; the draft names its own cast).
Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

INT. THE HIVE, NOODLE SHOP STOREROOM - NIGHT #13#

The Hive: a dense, self-built block of stacked homes and workshops, forgotten by the city that grew up around it. We don't see it whole yet. Only this.

A tiny storeroom behind a six-seat noodle counter. Sacks of flour. Crates of green onions. A single bare bulb. Above, close enough to touch, the underside of the elevated railway.

On a thin futon between the flour sacks: Mara. Pale, unwashed, wearing an old cardigan that isn't hers.

The curtain moves. KANEKO (70s), tiny, sharp-eyed, brings in a bowl of noodles, steam rising into the bulb light.

KANEKO
(in Japanese)
Eat.

MARA
(in halting Japanese)
Thank you. Has anyone... come? Asking?

KANEKO
(in Japanese)
Nobody comes here who isn't lost.

She sets the bowl down. Then, at the curtain, she pauses.

KANEKO (CONT'D)
(in Japanese)
Your father used to sit on the third stool. He always paid too much.
(beat)
And your sister always ate everything on his plate.

Mara almost laughs. It comes out wrong, too close to crying.

MARA
(in halting Japanese)
She told me about this place. All the time. When we were kids. I thought it was gone.

KANEKO
(in Japanese)
Everything is going. We are just slower.

Kaneko goes. The curtain falls shut.

Mara eats three mouthfuls. Stops.

She reaches under the flour sacks and takes out her phone. Looks at it a long time. Then she pulls the futon cover over her head, a child hiding under a blanket, and turns it on.

Under the cover, the screen lights her face blue.

ON THE SCREEN: 31 MISSED CALLS - VERA.

She presses play on the newest voicemail and holds the phone to her ear.

VERA (V.O.)
(filtered)
You left your umbrella. You'll get soaked.
(beat)
Just call me. You don't have to say anything. Just call, so I know.

Mara's thumb hovers over CALL.

A TRAIN thunders overhead. The bulb swings. Flour dust sifts down through the light like slow snow.

Her thumb doesn't move.

The train passes. Silence.

She turns the phone off. Pushes it deep under the flour sacks. Lies down with her back to the room.

In her fist, the coin-locker key.

HOLD on the swinging bulb as it slowly comes to rest.
