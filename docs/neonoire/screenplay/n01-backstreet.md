>THE LOOK<

Tokyo as a memory that is still happening.

The city in this film belongs to no single year. Pay phones beside smartphones. Boxy CRT televisions glowing in shop windows. Showa-era vending machines humming on empty corners beneath new glass towers. None of it is ever commented on. It is simply how the world is.

LIGHT. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel like something remembered.

RAIN. Never glamorous. No lightning, no storms. Cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective.

CAMERA. Wide and patient. Close-ups are rare, so they count. We watch people inside the city, not apart from it.

SOUND. Real Tokyo sounds, placed slightly wrong: a crossing melody for an empty street, a television laughing in an empty room. Music arrives late, and sparingly.

OTHER LIVES. There is always a sign of other people -- a lit window, a radio through a wall, laundry overhead. Every private moment happens among thousands of others.

!NOTHING IS EXPLAINED.

LANGUAGE. Everything is spoken in English, whatever language the characters would be speaking.

REFERENCE POINTS. Ozu's TOKYO STORY for restraint. KOWLOON GENERIC ROMANCE and the 1997 illustrated Kowloon Walled City panoramas for density, texture and memory.

FADE IN:

EXT. BACKSTREET, KANDA - NIGHT #1#

.INSERT - THE HIVE, REMEMBERED

Night. Rain. An eleven-storey block of balconies and wires wedged between glass towers, a railway curling around its shoulder, a hundred small windows lit amber and green. Over it, an old woman's voice, unhurried, speaking to us in English.

KANEKO (V.O.)
This is how I remember it.

KANEKO (V.O.)
After the war they raised a block beside the railway, to house the factories. The factories closed. We stayed.

KANEKO (V.O.)
We built upward. A room for a daughter. A shop for a brother. A stair, a pipe, a balcony bolted onto a balcony. Sixty years, until nobody could say where the factory ended and we began.

KANEKO (V.O.)
The city grew up around us, and forgot we were here. That was all right. We were busy.

KANEKO (V.O.)
Now the land is worth more than we are. Everything is going. We are just slower.

.BACK TO SCENE

Rain. Not a storm. Just cold, steady, patient rain.

A narrow street of shuttered shops. A vending machine hums on the corner. High up, one lit window, a radio playing behind it.

MARA VOSS (24) walks fast, arms folded, soaked, no umbrella. Her phone lights up: VERA. She watches it ring.

MARA
(to the phone)
Not tonight.

She declines it.

.INSERT - THE SCRAP

The note in her hands. Her handwriting, in biro: 1.00 Sakai. Underneath is an address. Beneath that, underlined twice: "just listen."

.BACK TO SCENE

She looks down the lane. At the far end, the bar sign. She checks her watch (12.45). Early.

The rain thickens. She slips into the barbershop doorway, behind the scooter and the crates, and presses back into the shadow to shelter from the rain. The beer crates will hide her from anyone in the lane. She hopes. She isn't sure she wants to walk in at all.

Somewhere nearby, a pedestrian crossing CHIMES its old melody for an empty intersection. Over and over. No one crosses.

Footsteps. Hurried. Uneven.

An OLD MAN (70s) in a cheap raincoat and a grey flat cap comes in from the road, one hand pressed to his side as if something is hidden there. He keeps looking back.

Mara sees him. Looks at the scrap.

She leans out of the shadow. The red bird clip catches the street light.

He sees it. He stops, six metres short of her. For one second his whole face changes.

Then he shakes his head. Barely. A man refusing a stranger. And he turns his back on her, slowly, to face the road.

Behind him, at the mouth of the lane, headlights. A black sedan pulls across the opening and stops, blocking it. Its high beams shine straight down the lane, lighting the rain in long white columns.

Two MEN get out and walk in, black silhouettes against the glare. Black clothes. Masks. They don't run.

The old man doesn't move. He stands between them and Mara's doorway, a small figure in the white light, his shadow stretching long down the lane toward her.

OLD MAN
(to himself)
After twenty years.

A suppressed SHOT. Flat and ordinary, like a door closing.

The old man folds onto the street.

One of the masked men touches his ear.

MASKED MAN
First position done. Moving to second.

They walk back to the sedan. They don't hurry. They never look at the doorway. It pulls away and takes the long way round the block, its high beams swinging off the lane.

Mara runs to the old man. Her bag slides off her shoulder and spills across the wet street. She doesn't notice.

MARA
(whispering)
Mr. Sakai?

MARA
It's okay -- I'll get someone --
(haltingly)
Ambulance. I'll call. Ambulance.

His hand comes out of his coat with something small and brass in it. He presses it into her palm and closes her fingers over it.

OLD MAN
Don't let them have it.

MARA
(haltingly)
Have what? I don't --

He's gone. Rain in his open eyes.

In her hand: a small brass key on a stamped number tag.

At the other end of the street, the amber glow of a bar. Mara runs for it.

Behind her, on the street: her purse, open in the rain.
