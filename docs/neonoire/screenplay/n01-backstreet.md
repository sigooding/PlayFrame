>THE LOOK<

Tokyo as a memory that is still happening.

The city in this film belongs to no single year. Pay phones beside smartphones. Boxy CRT televisions glowing in shop windows. Showa-era vending machines humming on empty corners beneath new glass towers. None of it is ever commented on. It is simply how the world is.

LIGHT. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel like something remembered.

RAIN. Never glamorous. No lightning, no storms. Cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective.

CAMERA. Wide and patient. Close-ups are rare, so they count. We watch people inside the city, not apart from it.

SOUND. Real Tokyo sounds, placed slightly wrong: a crossing melody for an empty street, a television laughing in an empty room. Music arrives late, and sparingly.

OTHER LIVES. There is always a sign of other people -- a lit window, a radio through a wall, laundry overhead. Every private moment happens among thousands of others.

!NOTHING IS EXPLAINED.

LANGUAGE. The Voss sisters speak English to each other. Dialogue marked (in Japanese) is spoken in Japanese and subtitled in English.

REFERENCE POINTS. Ozu's TOKYO STORY for restraint. KOWLOON GENERIC ROMANCE and the 1997 illustrated Kowloon Walled City panoramas for density, texture and memory.

FADE IN:

EXT. BACKSTREET, KANDA - NIGHT #1#

Rain. Not a storm. Just cold, steady, patient rain.

A narrow street of shuttered shops that could belong to any year of the last forty. Hand-painted signs. Tangled wires overhead. At the corner, an old vending machine throws cold white light across the wet asphalt, rain falling through it in columns.

Its HUM is the loudest thing here.

MARA VOSS (24), American, walks fast, arms folded, no umbrella. Hair soaked flat. She has been crying, or she is about to.

Her phone BUZZES. The screen: VERA.

She looks at it. Lets it ring. Declines.

It buzzes again at once. VERA.

MARA
(to the phone)
Not tonight.

She switches it off and shoves it into her purse.

The rain thickens. She ducks into the recessed doorway of a closed barbershop, its striped pole dark and still, and presses back into the shadow. Going nowhere.

Somewhere nearby, a pedestrian crossing CHIMES its old melody for an empty intersection. Over and over. No one crosses.

Footsteps. Hurried. Uneven.

An OLD MAN (70s) in a cheap raincoat, one hand pressed to his side as if something is hidden there. He keeps looking back.

He passes Mara's doorway without seeing her.

Headlights sweep the wet street. A black sedan rolls in and stops without hurry.

Two MEN step out. Black clothes. Masks. They don't run.

The old man stops. He doesn't turn around. He seems to know there is no point.

OLD MAN
(in Japanese; to himself)
Twenty years.

A suppressed SHOT. Flat and ordinary, like a door closing.

The old man folds to his knees, then onto his side in the black water.

In the doorway, Mara has both hands over her mouth.

One of the masked men touches his earpiece.

MASKED MAN
(in Japanese)
First position done. Moving to second.

They get back in the car. It pulls away, unhurried. Taillights smear red across the road and are gone.

Rain. The vending machine hum. The crossing melody, still playing for no one.

Mara doesn't move.

Then the old man COUGHS.

She stares. He is still alive.

Every instinct says run. She doesn't. She steps out into the rain and kneels beside him.

MARA
It's okay -- I'll get someone --
(in halting Japanese)
Ambulance. I'll call. Ambulance.

She fumbles for her phone. Switched off. Her hands shake too hard to turn it on.

The old man grips her wrist, stronger than he should be. He presses something small and cold into her palm and folds her fingers around it.

OLD MAN
(in Japanese)
Don't let them have it.

Mara's lips move, working through the words. She understands. Just.

MARA
(in halting Japanese)
Have what? I don't --

His grip loosens. His eyes stay open, on nothing.

She opens her hand.

.INSERT - THE KEY

A small numbered key on a worn plastic tag. A coin-locker key.

.BACK TO SCENE

Headlights. Coming back.

Mara runs.

Her purse strap snags on the barbershop pole and tears. The purse drops into a puddle behind her. She doesn't stop.

The sedan pulls up beside the body. One masked man gets out and searches the old man's coat, methodically. Nothing.

His flashlight drifts across the street -- and stops on the purse lying in the water.
