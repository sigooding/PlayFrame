NEONOIRE
SCREENPLAY — SCENE 14 — INT. MARA'S APARTMENT

INT. MARA'S APARTMENT - DAY
Source: the final screenplay (Neonoire (3).fountain, September 2026), reproduced verbatim below its own heading.
Cast: Vera Voss, Jack.
Grammar: 35mm for the room, 50mm on the sketchbook.

INT. MARA'S APARTMENT - DAY #14#

A tiny studio in an old building. Grey daylight. Clothes on every surface. Sketches taped to the walls: pencil, charcoal, cheap watercolour. Trains. Rooftops. Laundry lines. Faces on the subway.

The room smells of someone who meant to come back.

Vera stands just inside the door. She hasn't taken her coat off. Jack moves through the room slowly, touching nothing yet. Looking.

JACK
When were you last here?

VERA
A week ago. She made me coffee. It was terrible.

Jack opens the fridge. Milk. A half-eaten cake in a box. He closes it.

JACK
She wasn't planning to leave.

VERA
No.

He crouches by the futon. A suitcase underneath, packed and zipped. He looks at it, then at Vera.

VERA (CONT'D)
That was for America. She'd booked a flight. Next month.

JACK
You didn't want her to go.

VERA
I didn't want her to run away again.
(beat)
That's what I said. The night she left. That she always runs.

She looks at the wall of drawings, not at him.

VERA (CONT'D)
It was about Dad. It's always about Dad. She wanted to dig it all up. Who he was here. Why he --
(beat)
I told her to leave him dead.
(beat)
She said I was the one who ran. All the way back here, chasing a ghost, and I won't even look at it.
(beat)
I told her to go, then. Go home. Those were the last things I said to her.

Jack says nothing. He knows better than to comfort it away.

On the desk: a thick sketchbook, its cover soft with use. Jack opens it.

Page after page of the same place. A tiny noodle counter under a railway. Six stools. A curtain. A hand-painted sign. Drawn over and over, from different angles, in different years of her hand -- a child's crayon version taped inside the cover, the newest in careful ink.

JACK
She drew this a lot.

Vera glances over.

VERA
Since she was little. It's from a story I used to tell her. About the noodle shop Dad took us to. She was too young to remember it, so I told her.
(small smile)
She made it up from what I said. It's not real.

Jack turns a page. The sign, in careful ink. Every stroke of the kanji correct.

He has seen this sign. Twice this week. Once in a photograph on Vera's shelf. Once in a box in his own cupboard.

JACK
Can I borrow this?

VERA
Why?

JACK
People draw the places they want to go.

She hesitates, then nods. He closes the sketchbook and puts it under his arm, and says nothing else about it.
