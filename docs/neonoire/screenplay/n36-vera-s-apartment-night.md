Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

INT. VERA'S APARTMENT - NIGHT #36#

Vera on the floor with her back against the bed, the apartment dark around her. Her phone rings. Unknown number. She answers fast.

VERA
Mara?

JACK (V.O.)
It's me.

A beat. Disappointment, then something else.

VERA
Where are you? You sound like you're calling from 1985.

JACK
The countryside. There's no signal. I'm on a payphone. It's pink.

She almost laughs.

VERA
Did you find anything?

Jack looks at the receipts in his hand.

JACK
Maybe. I'll know tomorrow.

VERA
Come back safe.

JACK
It's just an inn. There's an old couple and a baseball game.

VERA
Come back safe anyway.

He hears her breathing on the line. Neither of them hangs up.

The coins run out. The line goes dead.

Vera looks at the phone. Jack looks at the payphone. Both of them hold on a moment longer than they need to.
