Cast: — (not boarded; the draft names its own cast).
Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

INT. TINY BREAKFAST COUNTER, NEAR THE STATION - MORNING #25A#

A coffee counter no wider than a corridor. Six seats, a glass siphon bubbling over a flame, toast cut as thick as a paperback. A CRT on the shelf shows a morning cooking show, sound low. The window is fogged from the inside.

Vera sits with her coat still on and her phone face up beside her, already looking at the door as though the day has started without her.

Jack sets a plate down in front of her: thick toast, a boiled egg, a small dish of pickled cabbage that nobody asked for.

VERA
What's this?

JACK
Breakfast.

VERA
I don't eat breakfast.

JACK
You drink coffee until the afternoon and then you forget to eat.

VERA
Has someone been reporting on me?

He says nothing. She looks at the plate. She looks at him. She picks up the toast, mostly to end the conversation.

VERA (CONT'D)
You sound like my sister.

Jack's hand stops on his cup.

Vera doesn't see it. She is looking at the cooking show.

VERA (CONT'D)
She used to stand over me with a plate. Twenty-four years old, bossing me around my own kitchen.
(beat)
She'd have liked you. She likes anyone who's stubborn.

JACK
She sounds like she was right.

VERA
Was.
(beat)
Is. I keep saying was.

JACK
(quietly)
Is.

She bites the toast. Chews. Her face changes, the way a person's face does when she finds out she was hungry.

She eats the egg. She eats the cabbage. On the television a chef lifts a lid and steam fills the frame.

Jack watches her eat, the way he watched her sleep. It is the easiest hour he has spent in twenty years, and he knows exactly what it is costing.

She wipes her fingers on a paper napkin.

VERA
Thank you.

JACK
For what?

VERA
For making me.

She reaches for her purse. He is already at the till, paying. Too much. The woman behind the counter doesn't mention it.
