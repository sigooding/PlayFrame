NOBODY'S WITNESS
SCREENPLAY — SCENE 10 — INT. JACK'S OFFICE

INT. JACK'S OFFICE - NIGHT
Source: the final screenplay (Neonoire (3).fountain, September 2026), reproduced verbatim below its own heading.
Cast: Jack, Vera Voss, Daniel Voss.
Grammar: 24mm for the room, 50mm on the desk, 85mm on Jack and the photograph.

INT. JACK'S OFFICE - NIGHT #10#

Second floor of an old building pressed against the elevated railway. One room. Frosted glass door, JACK hand-lettered on it in English and in katakana.

A desk. A black rotary phone. Filing cabinets. A small CRT television on the cabinet, muted, playing an old black-and-white samurai film. A sofa that has clearly been slept on.

A TRAIN passes outside. The blinds shiver. A spoon rattles in an empty cup. Then silence again.

JACK (48) sits at the desk eating a convenience-store rice ball, watching the silent swordsmen. Handsome once, in a way he stopped maintaining. A good coat, badly kept. He flicks an old steel lighter open and shut, open and shut. There are no cigarettes anywhere.

A knock on the frosted glass. Vera comes in, wet, the blue umbrella dripping.

VERA
Are you the one they call Jack?

JACK
Who calls me that?

VERA
Mr. Okada. The bar in Kanda.

Jack puts the rice ball down.

JACK
Sit down.

She sits. He doesn't switch to English. He lets her speak in the language she chose. She notices.

VERA
My sister has been missing for four days. The police say to go home and wait.

JACK
I don't do missing persons. People who go missing usually want to.

She places the red bird clip on his desk.

VERA
She was in that bar. The night two men were shot there.

Jack looks at the clip for a long moment. The lighter stops.

JACK
What did the police tell you?

VERA
To go home.

JACK
It's good advice.

VERA
Is it yours, too?

He doesn't answer. Another train passes. The blinds shiver.

JACK
Do you have photographs of her?

She takes an envelope from her bag. As she pulls out the photos, something slides out with them and falls face up on the desk:

The old framed photograph from her shelf, taken out of its frame. Twenty years ago. The American man in the rumpled suit. The two small girls. The noodle shop sign behind them.

Jack looks at it.

He does not move. On the TV behind him, a sword is drawn in total silence.

VERA
That's us. Before we left.

She has slipped into English. So does he, quietly.

JACK
Your father.

VERA
He died. That year. Here.

Jack picks the photograph up by its edges, carefully, the way you'd handle evidence. Or something fragile. He looks a moment too long at the noodle shop sign. Then he sets it down, face up, and squares it to the edge of the desk.

JACK
I'm sorry.

VERA
It was a long time ago.

JACK
Yes.

A beat.

JACK (CONT'D)
Ten thousand yen a day. Plus expenses. I'll need a key to her apartment, her phone number, her friends, where she worked, where she drank.

VERA
So you'll look.

JACK
I'll look.

She gathers the photographs. Leaves the red bird clip where it is. He doesn't give it back, and she doesn't ask.

At the door, she stops.

VERA
Mr. Okada said you found his daughter.

JACK
I found her.

VERA
Was she alive?

Jack looks at her. The honest answer is in the pause.

JACK
Her Japanese. Your sister's. Is it good?

VERA
Some. Badly.

JACK
Then she stands out. That helps.

Vera nods. Goes. Through the frosted glass, her shape goes down the stairs and is gone.

Jack sits very still.
