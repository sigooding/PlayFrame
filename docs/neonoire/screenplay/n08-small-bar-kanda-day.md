INT. SMALL BAR, KANDA - DAY #8#

The same bar in flat grey daylight. It looks smaller without the night. Chairs up on the counter. The CRT television is dark.

Where the journalist fell, there is a new rubber floor mat, too clean for this room.

OKADA (60s), the bar's owner, mops around it without looking at it. He has the stillness of a man who hasn't slept.

The door opens. Vera, the blue umbrella in her hand.

OKADA
(in Japanese; not looking up)
We're closed.

Vera holds up her phone: a photo of Mara, laughing at a party, a paper crown on her head.

VERA
(in Japanese)
My sister. Four nights ago. Was she here?

Okada looks at the photo for exactly one second.

OKADA
(in Japanese)
No.

VERA
(in Japanese)
You didn't look.

OKADA
(in Japanese)
I looked. No.

His eyes go, just once, to the far end of the counter. Then back to the mop.

Vera sees it. She doesn't push. She bows, small and polite, and leaves.

She has left the blue umbrella leaning against a stool.

Okada looks at the umbrella for a long moment.
