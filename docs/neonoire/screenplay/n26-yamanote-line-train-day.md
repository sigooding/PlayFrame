INT. YAMANOTE LINE TRAIN - DAY

Grammar: 50mm at seated height; the city circles past.

INT. YAMANOTE LINE TRAIN - DAY #26#

A green-striped commuter train circling the city. Rain on the windows. Afternoon light, grey and soft. The carriage is half empty: schoolkids, an old man asleep, a woman with shopping.

Vera and Jack sit side by side. Nowhere to go. Just riding the loop.

VERA
Dad used to do this when he couldn't think. Ride the whole loop. An hour. He said Tokyo was a clock and the Yamanote was the second hand.

JACK
Did it help?

VERA
I don't know. He did it a lot at the end.

The train sways. Her shoulder settles against his.

She takes his hand without asking and turns it over. The old steel lighter is in his palm; he has been flicking it without knowing.

She takes it. Opens it. Closes it. Opens it.

VERA (CONT'D)
You don't smoke.

JACK
I quit.

VERA
When?

JACK
Twenty years ago.

VERA
And you still carry the lighter.

JACK
Habit.

VERA
That's not a habit. That's a promise you don't trust yourself to keep.

He looks at her. She has no idea how true that is.

She tries to give it back. He closes her fingers around it.

JACK
Keep it.

VERA
Why?

JACK
Because I quit.

She looks at the lighter in her hand. Then she puts it in her coat pocket. And her head comes to rest on his shoulder, as if it has always belonged there.

Out of the window, Tokyo circles past. Rooftops. Laundry. Rain.
