EXT. THE HIVE - MORNING
Source: the final screenplay (Neonoire (3).fountain, September 2026), reproduced verbatim below its own heading. Written, not boarded: the numbered shot board covers the opening scenes only.
Cast: — (not boarded; the draft names its own cast).
Grammar: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening. The city lights the characters, not the sky: vending machines, shop signs, train windows, fluorescent 
tubes. Sodium orange against a sick fluorescent green. Soft halation around every light, blacks slightly crushed. It should look like film, and feel 
like something remembered. Rain is never glamorous — no lightning, no storms, cold, steady, patient rain that turns the streets black and reflective. 
Wide and patient; close-ups are rare, so they count. Nothing is explained.

EXT. THE HIVE - MORNING #97#

Rain.

In front of the Hive, a white ceremonial tent. Rows of folding chairs. A podium. Officials in dark suits. A giant banner of the computer rendering: glass towers, the little plaza, the fountain. TOMORROW'S TOKYO. A silver shovel on a stand, tied with a red-and-white ribbon, waiting for the ground-breaking.

Across the street, a small electronics shop. In its window, a row of televisions, old CRTs and new flat screens side by side, all showing the same morning news: a photograph of Kurose, and a front page of the Toto Shimbun held up to the camera.

People on the pavement slow down to watch. Then stop.

Among them, beside Kaneko, under a plain umbrella: Vera.

A black car pulls up at the tent. An AIDE hurries round with a large umbrella and opens the rear door.

Kurose steps out. Immaculate. He buttons his jacket. He turns toward the podium.

Four men in plain dark suits are standing between him and it. PROSECUTORS. One of them holds up an identification card and says something we can't hear.

Kurose looks at them. For a moment, nothing happens at all.

Then the lead prosecutor gestures toward a plain grey car at the kerb.

The aide steps forward with the umbrella. The prosecutor shakes his head, very slightly.

The aide lowers it.

Kurose walks from the tent to the grey car. Twenty metres. In the rain. No umbrella.

The rain soaks his silver hair flat. It darkens the shoulders of his beautiful suit. By the time he reaches the car, he looks like what he is: an old man.

Cameras flash. The CRTs in the shop window show the same moment, a second behind, over and over.

Vera watches the whole twenty metres. She does not look away.

Kaneko takes Vera's hand.

Further down the street, alone, collar up: Jack. Watching too.

Vera sees him. He sees her.

Across the street, through the rain and the camera flashes, neither of them moves.

The grey car pulls away. Behind it, the silver shovel stands on its stand, untouched, its ribbon going dark in the rain.
