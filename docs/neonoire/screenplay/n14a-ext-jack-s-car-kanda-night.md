NOBODY'S WITNESS
SCREENPLAY — SCENE 14A — INT./EXT. JACK'S CAR, KANDA

INT./EXT. JACK'S CAR, KANDA - NIGHT
Source: the final screenplay (Neonoire (3).fountain, September 2026), reproduced verbatim below its own heading.
Cast: Vera Voss, Jack.
Grammar: Through the windscreen and the mirror: two people in a car in the rain, looking at the same lane.

INT./EXT. JACK'S CAR, KANDA - NIGHT #14A#

Rain. Wipers. Kanda's wet streets slide past the windscreen. From the rear-view mirror, on a loop of string, the red bird clip swings with each turn.

In the mirror, behind it: a pair of headlights. The same distance, every turn. Never closer, never farther.

Jack watches them. One hand goes into his coat pocket and closes around the lighter. He takes a corner a shade too fast, then slow. The headlights follow, dipped, patient.

He stops at the mouth of the lane, exactly where the sedan stopped, and kills the engine and lights.

Forty metres back, the headlights slow. Stop. Go out.

He waits, long enough to be sure. Then he gets out and walks back along the wet road toward the dark car, one hand in his pocket.

A small, boxy, maroon hatchback, its glass steamed. He raps on the driver's window with a knuckle.

It winds down. Vera, both hands on the wheel.

He takes his hand from his pocket. The old steel lighter, open, unlit.

VERA
Is that what you were holding?

JACK
It's what I had.

He snaps it shut.

JACK (CONT'D)
Forty metres. Twenty minutes. You never once lost me.

VERA
Is that bad?

JACK
It's what a professional does.

VERA
Who did you think I was?

JACK
Somebody who'd be worse company.

VERA
You said you'd look. You didn't say where. I waited outside your office.

JACK
If you can follow me, so can they.

She hadn't thought of that. She looks past him at the empty road.

VERA
(quietly)
I brought food.

He sighs the sigh of a man who has lost an argument he was never allowed to make.

JACK
Park round the corner, then get in mine. One car is a man waiting. Two is somebody watching.

LATER. Engine off. Rain on the windscreen, no wipers; the glass turns the lane into smears of light. At the far end, small and amber, the bar sign.

Vera shakes the blue umbrella out onto his floor. From a paper bag, two convenience-store rice balls. She holds one out.

VERA
You were eating one when I came in. I assumed it was a lifestyle.

JACK
This is a stakeout. Nothing happens.

VERA
Then I won't miss anything.

He takes it. Their fingers don't touch. Both notice that they don't.

They watch the lane.

VERA (CONT'D)
What are we waiting for?

JACK
Whoever comes back.

VERA
Back?

JACK
Someone came back to this street that night. Nobody comes back for what they already have.

VERA
What didn't they have?

JACK
I don't know yet.

A TRAIN passes somewhere behind them. The mirror trembles, and the red bird swings. Vera watches it.

VERA
Is that for luck?

JACK
It's so I don't lose it.

A long silence. The rice ball in her lap, unwrapped, uneaten.

VERA
I told her she always runs.

JACK
You said.

VERA
It's the last thing I --

JACK
Runners don't pack. They leave with what they're wearing. Your sister had a suitcase zipped under the bed and a flight booked home.
(beat)
Whatever happened to her, she wasn't running from you.

VERA
You don't know that.

JACK
No.
(beat)
But I'd bet on it.

She looks at him for the first time since she sat down. He is looking at the lane. He doesn't look back.

A figure at the road end, under a black umbrella tilted low, face lost beneath it. He walks past the car (both of them sink an inch in their seats) and into the lane.

He goes to the bar's shuttered door. Tries the handle. Stands under the amber sign, looking up and down the lane. Then he turns back toward the road, and the umbrella swings toward the car.

Jack's arm goes out across Vera, pressing her back into the seat. Not looking. Reflex.

The umbrella holds on them. Then turns away. The man walks to a small white car further up the road, and gets in. He doesn't start it. He sits.

Jack looks at his own arm, still across her chest, as if it belonged to someone else. He takes it back.

They sit very still. After a long time, the white car pulls out and goes, without hurry. Jack finds a pencil stub, smooths the paper bag flat on his knee, and writes the plate number on it.

VERA
(quietly)
Is that routine? Watching a bar after a shooting?

JACK
Probably.

He folds the bag once and puts it inside his coat.

VERA
You don't think so.

JACK
I think it's a plate number.

3:40 a.m. The amber sign at the far end goes dark. Jack starts the engine.

JACK (CONT'D)
Call me when you go somewhere.

VERA
I will.

JACK
That's a lie.

VERA
Yes.

She sets the rice ball on his dashboard, still untouched, and gets out.

In the mirror he watches the blue umbrella go up the wet road and round the corner, and the dark take it.

A small engine starts, out of sight. Headlights swing across the end of the road and away. Jack counts ten. Then he pulls out after them, forty metres back. Never closer. Never farther.
