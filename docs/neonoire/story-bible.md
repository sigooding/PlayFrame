## Part 1 — What the film is

**Logline.** In a Tokyo that never quite left the 1980s, an American woman hires a private detective
to find her missing sister, not knowing he was the young policeman who helped bury the truth about
her father's death twenty years ago — and that his silence is about to cost her everything again.

**In one line (the director's style):** a Wong Kar-wai love story, shot with Ozu's patience,
interrupted by Kitano's violence, in a Tokyo that only exists in memory.

**The core.** Not the conspiracy. The feeling: two people falling in love in a city that is about
to disappear, and losing each other anyway. The crime plot gives urgency; the heart is longing,
regret, and "nostalgia for a life that never quite existed."

**Three layers.**
1. Love story — Vera and Jack.
2. Family mystery — Daniel Voss, Jack's past, the sisters.
3. Noir conspiracy — the Shiohama fire, Kurose, the killings, the Hive.

## Part 2 — Tone, look and references

**Visual identity: Tokyo as a memory that is still happening.** The city belongs to no single year:
payphones beside smartphones, CRTs in shop windows, Showa-era vending machines under glass towers.
Never commented on.

**Rules of the look (from the script's "The Look" page):**
1. The city lights the characters, never the sky (vending machines, signs, train windows, tubes).
2. Rain only shows where light passes through it. Never glamorous, no lightning, no storms.
3. Wide and patient; close-ups rare so they count. Figures small, the city large.
4. Always a sign of other lives (a lit window, a radio through a wall, laundry).
5. Ordinary moments get full screen time.
6. Nothing is explained.
7. Places outlive feelings: locations stay the same while people change. Key places are seen twice,
   first together, then after the loss (noodle counter, train, crossing, rooftop).

**Reference split (each has one job):**
- *Kowloon Walled City illustrated* (Iwanami, 1997) — the world: density, cross-sections, clutter,
  "a place full of life, seen after the life has gone."
- *Kowloon Generic Romance* (Jun Mayuzuki) — the feeling: nostalgia, longing, memory. Borrow the
  mood, not the setup (its heroine works in real estate inside the city; ours is the destruction of
  the city seen from outside).
- *Tokyo Story* (Ozu) — the behaviour: restraint, grief without theatrics, ordinary life continuing.
- Noir — the pressure trying to destroy all of it.
- Comparable films named in development: Wong Kar-wai (*Chungking Express*, *Fallen Angels*, *In the
  Mood for Love*), Shunji Iwai (*Swallowtail Butterfly*), Takeshi Kitano (*Hana-bi*, *Sonatine* —
  stillness broken by flat, sudden violence), Michael Mann (*Thief*, *Collateral* — calm
  professional killers), *Lost in Translation*.

**Warning recorded in development:** the director sometimes pulls toward bigger genre beats and
80s/90s action exaggeration. Keep quiet as the default and violence as the exception, or the film
tips into a standard thriller.

**Sound.** Real Tokyo sounds placed slightly wrong: a crossing melody for an empty street, a TV
laughing in an empty room, a vending machine thanking no one. Music arrives late and sparingly.
The crossing melody "Tōryanse" (a gate you may not return through) was suggested for its meaning.
Neighbour's-radio song: an original 80s-style city-pop ballad, "Wasuremono" ("Something Left
Behind"), about a forgotten umbrella and a phone that never rings — Suno prompt and Japanese lyrics
were written. It plays through Vera's wall (sc 4, 65), on the lounge piano (sc 72), and at Kaneko's
new counter (sc 100). License risk: famous city-pop tracks are expensive; write an original.

**The Hive's look is the one exception** to the plain Ozu look (see Part 5).

## Part 3 — Characters

**VERA VOSS (29).** American. Lived in Tokyo from about 4 to 9 while her father worked there; came
back as an adult because it is the only place that ever felt like home. Japanese fluent but careful,
learned as a child and relearned. Carries guilt about the last fight with Mara. Falls in love with
Jack. Arc: from not looking (she threw the letter away) to looking; she faces Kurose herself.
- Name history: first "Hayashi" (rejected: the parents are American), then "Hale", then **Voss**
  (director's choice).

**MARA VOSS (24).** American, Vera's younger sister; was 4 when they left Tokyo and remembers it only
through Vera's stories — she draws Kaneko's noodle counter from those stories all her life. Brave:
she went to meet Sakai alone. Japanese poor. Hides in Kaneko's storeroom. Dies in scene 71. Last
words: "Tell her I'm sorry" (chosen over "Don't let her know", "Is she okay?", a childhood nickname
and silence; the fight gives the "sorry" its meaning).

**JACK (48).** Private investigator. Twenty years ago he was a young Tokyo police detective assigned
to cases involving foreigners because he spoke English; colleagues called him "Jack" and it stuck.
His real name is never used in the script. Daniel Voss brought him the arson case; Jack believed him,
became almost his friend, ate at Kaneko's with him — then was ordered to close it and **signed the
report calling the fire an accident**. Weeks later Daniel was dead. Jack left the force and never
left the city.
- **Fatal flaw: protective silence.** He was silent twenty years ago, and repeats it with Mara and
  Vera. His arc ends when he speaks on the record, including his own signature (sc 82).
- Carries a steel lighter though he quit smoking twenty years ago ("a promise you don't trust
  yourself to keep"); gives it to Vera.
- **History of the character's nationality (important):** development first made Jack Japanese
  (nickname "Jack") precisely so he could have been a Tokyo police detective; other options were a
  Japanese-American who naturalized to join the force, or an American journalist (rejected — the
  director wanted Jack as the detective). PlayFrame later recast Jack as **a white American** (board
  note, 25 September 2026). See Part 10: the backstory now needs reconciling.

**DANIEL VOSS (41 at death).** American insurance investigator at the Tokyo office of an American
insurer. Sent to sign off on the Shiohama fire claim; proved it was arson. Pressured by his own
company, the police and Kurose's money. Found dead, ruled a suicide. His notebook forgives Jack ("I
don't blame him") and ends: "If something happens to me, it will not be what they say. Tell Vera."
- Earlier ideas dropped: Daniel as a Tokyo police detective (impossible once the family was
  American).

**DETECTIVE ISHIDA (50s).** Gentle, tired, excellent English; Jack's senior twenty years ago who told
him to sign; the Chairman's man ever since. Earns Jack's trust by telling him true things. The
audience learns he is the traitor after the roadside inn (sc 51) — suspense over surprise — while
Jack does not. "You always tell the wrong person." Ends in the back seat of Kurose's car, drinking
the tea (sc 93–96). Found dead the next morning, sitting up in his own car under the expressway, no marks;
his "statement" (the report he was writing in careful longhand in sc 80) says he acted alone. It is the
same ruling Daniel got: suicide. Motif: tea (the untouched cup and the wiped spill in sc 6; the flask in sc 94).

**KUROSE (70s), the Chairman.** Named in the full draft (placeholder earlier: "the Chairman").
Bubble-era developer who ordered the Shiohama fire; now chairman of KUROSE DEVELOPMENT, about to enter
public life. Calm, courteous, rarely seen. "Let the detective look. He was always very good at
finding things. It's what he does afterwards that he's bad at." "Good men are very expensive."
Taken away from the Hive ground-breaking by prosecutors (never called an arrest) and walked twenty metres
into the rain without an umbrella. Whether he is charged or freed is never said; Harada: "Men like that are
back at their desks by Friday." The plaza is built anyway.

**SAKAI (70s).** The old man in the cold open. Bubble-era land clearer (jiageya) who carried out the
fire. Kept a cassette of Kurose ordering it and Daniel's notebook for twenty years in rented locker
114 (see Part 10 on the number). Dying of cancer; wrote to Vera; arranged a handover at the bar to
Daniel's daughter and a journalist. Shot in the cold open.

**KANEKO (70s).** Runs the six-seat noodle counter in the Hive fifty years. Remembers Daniel ("He
always paid too much") and the girls. Hides Mara. Refuses to flee the raid. Opens a new counter under
the arches at the end, keeping the sign and the stools.

**OKADA (60s).** Owner of the small bar in Kanda where the journalist dies. Finds Mara's red bird
clip, gives it to Vera with Jack's card ("He found my daughter once"). Keeps the cassette in his cash
drawer.

**Others:** the JOURNALIST (Toto Shimbun), HARADA (his editor), the YOUNG OFFICER at the counter,
MR. and MRS. NODA (the roadside inn, both survive), MRS. SAKAI, the RADIO REPAIRMAN (pulls the main
switch), the OLD TOBACCONIST, the BARBER ("One car. Twice."), the MASKED LEADER and the hit team.

**The hit team.** Masked, anonymous, professional, from Kurose's private security firm (the burnt-out grey
car is registered to it) — NOT yakuza
(kept separate so yakuza imagery stays with the past). Eight men at the inn ("probably only 8 men,
otherwise too difficult to escape"). They never chase. Their tell: they say "Shitsurei shimasu" ("Excuse me") quietly before they act — to Sakai in the lane
(sc 1), entering the bar (sc 2), and the driver opening the car door (sc 70), which is how Mara knows him.
Their radio word is still "Position."

## Part 4 — The backstory (the conspiracy)

Bubble era. Kurose's developer company wanted an old apartment block (Shiohama) cleared; tenants
refused to sell. Sakai's crew brought flowers, then iron bars, then a fire started in two places at
once. People died. Ruled accidental. Daniel Voss, investigating the insurance claim, proved arson.
Jack (young detective) believed him, then signed the accident report on Ishida's instruction. Daniel
was found dead, ruled suicide. Sakai kept a cassette of Kurose ordering the fire ("then it will be
empty in a different way") and Daniel's notebook.

Now: Sakai is dying and Kurose is about to become untouchable. Sakai writes to Vera, arranges to hand
everything to Daniel's daughter and a journalist at a Kanda bar at 1:00. Kurose's team kills Sakai in
the lane and the journalist in the bar. Mara, who came to meet Sakai, gets the key.

Why the hit team needs Jack: they can't search the Hive (too many passages; residents see everyone),
so Kurose lets Jack look, planning to take "the girl and the thing" when Jack brings them out.

## Part 5 — Locations

**Tokyo** — present day that never left the Showa era (see Part 2).

**THE HIVE** — the film's Kowloon: a dense, self-built block the city grew around and forgot.
Where the romance lives, where Mara hides, and what Kurose wants demolished. The film is a love
story in a place about to disappear. Canon decided 28–29 September:
- Geometry: eleven-storey front tower; four-storey rear wing against the elevated viaduct, its roof
  level with the tracks; counter and storeroom at ground level under the viaduct.
- The only location with its own look: sixty years of retrofitted pipes and additions; a few small
  faded handmade neon signs; muted near-monochrome night (black, amber, steel blue, haze); murky
  grey-ochre day (its own weather). Every light belongs to someone who lives there; nothing
  futuristic. Kaneko's counter and storeroom stay warm tungsten — home.
- "Blade Runner" was the director's reference word; the decision was to take the 1982 film's
  retrofitted texture and lighting only, never its science fiction, and never to write the title in
  prompts. First version was too colourful; toned down.

**Vera's apartment** — one room, third floor of a plain 1970s block by the elevated line; the bed is
against the wall opposite the window; no sofa. Her building must never look like the Hive.

**Kanda backstreet** — a pedestrian lane too narrow for cars; the sedan blocks the alley mouth → script pass (Part 9 item 4) → three new Act Two scenes (25A, 27A, 63A).
Layout: car end → old man → barbershop doorway / vending machine → bar sign.

**The roadside inn** — a random, faded 1975 inn outside Tokyo with no hidden connection to the plot
("that starts making every location feel engineered"). Its quiet check-in scene secretly sets up
every escape beat (back door, stuck bathroom window, truck with keys).

**Others:** Jack's office (frosted JACK door, rotary phone, CRT, trains past the blinds); the police
station (clock one minute fast); Okada's bar; the all-night counter under the arches; the hotel
lounge (amber, Showa-era, where Vera waits); Kurose's 40th floor with the model under glass; Kato
Rental Lockers, Ueno; the Toto Shimbun newsroom; Jack's rooftop.

## Part 7 — Key scenes and their intent

**The rain scene (sc 74).** Restrained, Tokyo Story. Not a storm, cold relentless rain. She runs
badly, in heels and her mother's wine-red dress, and loses a shoe. "You knew." His silence is the
answer. Weak blows to his chest, not a cinematic slap. "She was waiting for me." "And I was waiting
for you. And you were with her." She folds, grips his coat, realises whose coat it is, pushes him
away. He kneels a few feet off and never touches her again. Extreme wide, held too long; sound drops
to rain; a reflection shows a slightly different street and maybe a figure with a blue umbrella;
a train of oblivious strangers passes; the score enters late and far away. Then Ozu cutaways.
- Why she's in the dress: she was waiting for Jack at the hotel lounge. He had asked her to dinner
  ("There's something I need to tell you") meaning the truth; she heard love. He was moving Mara.
- Rejected ideas: Jack deliberately making Vera hate him to protect her (dropped — his guilt is
  real, which is stronger); a big melodramatic breakdown.

**The cold open.** Two murders, one coordinated operation ("First position done. Moving to second").
Mara survives because the team is efficient, not careless: they assume Sakai is dead and have a
second target; she is hidden. Her purse falls, which is how they learn her name.

**The roadside inn.** Jack survives by noticing, not by being an action hero. Eight men, four cars.
The TV keeps playing baseball; a shot vending machine chimes "thank you"; the pink payphone rings and
is never answered or explained. The men watch the taillights and don't chase.

**The ending (revised 29 Sept).** Ishida is destroyed by his own side and his death is ruled a suicide with a
statement that clears Kurose. Kurose is walked into the rain by prosecutors, but the film stops short of an
arrest or a verdict; the Hive still comes down, the clean plaza and fountain are built (sc 99A), and Mara is
still dead — justice without restoration. Final
image deliberately ambiguous: a man's shadow in the doorway, Kaneko sets a second bowl, Vera doesn't
turn.
