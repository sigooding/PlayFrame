# NEONOIRE — the long-hold pass: ten new frames for the three scenes one board was carrying (4 October 2026)

**Ten generations, ten new numbered frames, nothing renumbered and nothing written over: 344–353.** The pass
takes the head of [the coverage note written after the first voiced animatic](../../neonoire/shots-needed.md) —
a single board cannot keep a long exchange alive, and the three longest holds in the film had one frame each:
**scene 23 at 95 seconds** on shot 199, **scene 22 at 64 seconds** on 197, **scene 20 at 55 seconds** on 194.
`build:neonoire` now reads **332/332 keyframes on disk, 0 placeholder cards**; the film's next free production
number is **354**.

The standing instruction for the round: *"if shot description don't make complete visual sense go by the logic
of the scene, keep character and scene consistancy, make sure any original image replaced is backed up."*
Nothing was replaced, so the archive rule did not fire: every one of the ten is a **new number at a new asset
path**. If a future retake replaces any of them, that study is archived before the new frame goes over it, as
always — and `verify:neonoire` now holds the ten's origin in place so a silent replacement cannot happen.

## Where each frame plays (the scene's logic, not the board order)

`story-order.mjs` places a frame by its own quoted line, and the pass wrote each quote from the beat it draws,
so the new frames interleave rather than trailing the scenes:

- **Scene 20** — 309, 193, 194, **345, 344**, 284, **346**: she pulls the book into her lap (345), *then* he
  sits down and makes himself smaller (344) — the draft's own order — then her small bitter laugh (284), then
  the bulb hums (346).
- **Scene 22** — 197, **347**, 285, 348, **349**, 198: the train and the trembling cups (347), the inside pocket
  (285), then her single (348) and his answer (349), then the cup moved back from the edge (198).
- **Scene 23** — 199, **350, 351, 352**, 200, **353**: he turns the skewer (350), Jack watches him (351), the
  napkin goes across the table (352), the train roars and the lanterns sway (200), then Jack nods (353).

## The three calls this pass made (permanent unless the director overrides)

1. **The new frames carry lens lines 50mm and 85mm inside 35mm scenes.** Scene 22's lens plan is "35mm for the
   arch, 50mm along the counter" and scene 23's is "35mm across the table"; the singles and the two table
   inserts go to 50mm and 85mm, as the coverage passes already do in scenes 11, 14 and 29. The masters 195,
   197 and 198 keep their 35mm/*50mm* framing untouched.
2. **350 and 352 are hands-and-object inserts with no faces in them.** The scene's power is that nothing is
   said plainly; the cost is that Ishida has no close single in the scene. If the director wants one, it is the
   next frame — the board can take it without disturbing these.
3. **353 is the last beat, not 200.** The page gives *A long pause. A train roars overhead. The lanterns sway.*
   and then *Jack nods slowly.* So 200 keeps the sway and 353 takes the nod, and the scene now ends on his face
   rather than on the lanterns — the choice the scene's own logic asks for, since the paragraph after the nod
   says the nod is what makes it dangerous. 353 is therefore generated from 198, so the lanterns' last position
   carries from 200 into the new final frame.

## Honest caveats (in the board notes as well)

- **The trembling cannot read in a still.** 347's train is carried by the quiver of the crockery and the swung
  tube; the animatic supplies the sound. If it reads as an ordinary counter at full size, that is the frame to
  re-shoot rather than re-time.
- **347's counter dress is busier than the master's.** The delivered frame shows the white bowls and chopsticks,
  the two tan paper cups *and* the white coffee cups; on set, pick one coffee service and stay with it.
- **348's lamp is warmer than 197's tile-green wide.** She is lit by the wall bulbs rather than the tubes — the
  correct reading for a reaction shot in this room, but the two frames must be graded together.
- **349 reads wider than the board's 50mm medium.** The study came back a broader single than the line asked
  for; the beat survives it (his stillness against her turned face), and it is logged rather than re-generated.
- **351's eyeline drifts past his partner** rather than holding him, and his rain-wet hair does not read. Both
  are set notes, not re-generation candidates.
- **352's napkin is partly legible pseudo-text.** The board says the address must not be readable; the study
  came back with character-like strokes. Dress the prop with writing that reads as a hand and not as words
  before print, or accept it as an artefact of the study — it is the one caveat in the pass worth a retake.
- **353's nod cannot be seen in a still.** The frame is the instant the nod begins; the animatic holds it, and
  the caveat is recorded rather than fixed.
- **351 and 353 are the same man in the same seat at the same table.** Their eyelines and the table's dress are
  cut together in the animatic; check them as a pair before approval, not separately.
- **The ten are first-generation studies.** They were reviewed at full size in this session against the masters
  they were generated from (contact sheet
  `public/images/neonoire/reviews/long-hold-344-353.jpg`), but production approval is the director's, as it has
  been for every pass.

## References attached, and what was not touched

masters and the identity sheets listed above. **Nothing on disk was overwritten**, so no archive version exists
for these ten — the archive keeps its 276 versioned studies of replaced work, and `verify:neonoire` now asserts
that 344–353 are *new* files, at 16:9, with the pass named in their notes. `rewrite-pending.mjs` stays empty;
`sync-scene6.mjs` was **not** re-run (its scene list is scenes 1, 2, 6 and 14A, and re-running it would drop the
new ids — see below).
