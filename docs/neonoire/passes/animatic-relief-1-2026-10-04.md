## The decisions this round made (permanent unless the director overrides)

1. **The next ten are the two unboarded beats first, then the animatic's list in order.** The 4
   October retake round recorded two beats the script names with no frame anywhere — scene 59's
   INTERCUT to Mrs. Sakai's empty house and scene 94's door closing. Script beats outrank pacing
   inserts, so they take 344–348 and the first five rows of the animatic's long-talking-scenes
   table (in screenplay order) take 349–353: one new frame per board, on the single beat each row
   names (Vera's single, Kaneko's bowl, the storeroom bulb, Jack on the sack, Vera at the counter).
2. **The INTERCUT is four cuts, not five.** The page writes five objects — the kotatsu, the altar,
   *the television on with the sound off*, the teacup, the phone — but a bare television screen has
   no business as its own still: the room wide already shows it on, glowing, and the empty room says
   everything a screen would add. So 344 carries the "sound off" beat and the series is four, all
   35mm across the cut on the scene's own grammar, playing before 228 in the page's order.
3. **The house is the scene 29 house with the people removed.** Mrs. Sakai is the widow of scene 29;
   "nobody is left to hear it" means the same room, the same drained interior — tatami beige, altar
   gold, grey daylight — emptied. The altar photograph is held to `s1/07-old-man.jpg` (the cold open's
   man, the one the film never names on screen), and 345 is the face 344's wide must agree with.
4. **348 is the hinge, not the walk or the sit.** 142 owns the stop in the rain and 143 owns the
   sitting, so the door frame is the getting-in and the closing: 50mm at the door on the scene's
   lens plan, cream leather and amber carried from `s94/141-he-drinks.jpg`, the street and its one
   lit window carried from `s94/139-only-tea.jpg`, Ishida NO TIE and soaked.
5. **One frame per long-talking board, the rest queued.** Each row of the animatic's table wants
   three to six shots; this pass gives each of its first five rows its single most-needed frame. The
   next pass takes **scene 23's 199 "You never call" first** (95 s on one board — the longest in the
   film; the list wants five or six shots: the bowls and chopsticks, Ishida's hands, Jack listening,
   the napkin address at the end, the train overhead), then scene 25's 202 and 258, scene 27A's 292
   and scene 29's 273, then the rest of the table, the below-30 s list, the song (74–77), the
   rewrite-changed frames (64, 36, 70) and the title/credit cards — all still standing in
   [shots-needed.md](../shots-needed.md).
6. **Nothing was replaced, so nothing was backed up.** All ten frames are new files at new numbers;
   no existing image moved. The backup rule is asserted vacuously: every pre-existing study is byte-
   identical to HEAD except the bundle, the boards and the scripts this pass touched.

## Honest caveats (all carried in the board notes)

- **344's altar photograph reads a different face** — younger, smiling, bearded — than the locked
  `s1/07-old-man.jpg` man 345 holds. The retake call (with the old man's face as reference) **hit the
  session's ten-generation limit and did not fire**; 344 stands installed with the caveat and is the
  head of the next pass's queue, to be regenerated against 345's photograph. The patchwork cover also
  reads busier than 273's quilted cover.
- **345's photograph sits on a low stand beside the altar shelf** rather than on the shelf itself.
- **346's tatami weave reads coarser** than the scene 29 mat, and its window is a bare glass pane
  rather than the room's curtained one (accepted as a different corner of the same room).
- **347 carries a dark water stain** on the left wall (read as the house's neglect) and the phone is
  a 1990s push-button model rather than a rotary (the film's no-single-year rule covers it).
- **348's door reads fully open** rather than mid-close, the lit second-floor window is not legible
  at this size in the background building, and Ishida's profile reads a shade younger than the sheet.
- **349's lower right holds an open dark suitcase** where 270's beat has the case zipped under the
  futon — crop it out or accept it on the cut; it reads as the scene's own case, not a second one.
- **350's counter wood reads a shade darker** than the master's.
- **351's dust ring reads a touch literal**, closer to a plate of flour than drifting dust.
- **352's plaster wall reads a touch warmer** than the scene 13 storeroom, and the shirt buttoned at
  the second button reads a shade neater than the sheet's open collar.
- **353's clock reads 10:10** rather than the scene's 11:40 (the same clock caveat 197 already
  carries — set it on the day), and the radio sits on the side shelf rather than behind the counter.
