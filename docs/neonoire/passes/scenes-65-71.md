## Locks

- **The wine-red dress** is one garment across the whole film: shot 234 holds it up from the box, shot 235 wears it at the bar, and both follow the wardrobe master `s72/69-the-wait.jpg` — broad straps, modest cowl neckline, calf-length bias skirt, low red court shoes. Vera is in her cream knit at home, dry groomed hair and rose-red lipstick at the lounge; only scene 73's rain ruins it. The pale-blue umbrella on the lounge stool is Mara's, borrowed at the apartment door.
- **The apartment** follows `s4/33-the-apartment.jpg` and `s36/208-come-back-safe-anyway.jpg`; the box's marker TOKYO is the mother's hand, and the old steel lighter goes into the bag after this shot.
- **The service road** is a new exterior master (`s67/234`): the pillars of `s74/73-two-small-figures.jpg`, the grey sedan of `s61/228-nine-oclock.jpg`, one sodium lamp. Its lit sign reads **8:52**, correct at full size. Shots 239's ambush and the unboarded gunfire play on this road.
- **Kaneko's storeroom** follows `s25/200-the-number-114.jpg`; **Kaneko** is held to `s86/123-fifty-years.jpg`, **Mara** to `sheets/mara.jpg` in the borrowed coat over the brown cardigan, cloth bag in hand.
- **The passages** follow `s85/120-the-hive-is-watching.jpg` and `s16/186-everybody-sees-him.jpg`; **the radio repairman** (carded since scene 85) nods from his bench in 238 and returns with the flashlight in 240; the family's father and his lowered bat cross between the same two shots.
- **The driver** of shot 239 is an uncarded Ishida man: plain, fifties, dark coat. His face locks here and nowhere else; no cast card is invented for him.

## Colour arc

The cross-cut is the point: the lounge (235) is the film's warmest room — amber, brass, bottles like a shrine — against the service road's single sodium lamp in blue-black (236, 239). Between them the Hive keeps its own grammar: tungsten kindness in the storeroom (237), sodium bulbs and sick green with warm door-rectangles in the passages (238), and in 240 the cold corridor green at floor level losing to a thousand warm windows waking above. Vera waits in amber while the trap waits in sodium; neither knows about the other.

## Caveats carried, all reviewed at full size

- **235:** the wall clock's hands read about 11:55, not the script's 9:58. Carried as a production-review check, exactly as scene 72's clock already is; the beat ("The clock: 10:00") is legible in the board either way.
- **237:** Kaneko's apron reads a shade darker than `s86/123`'s; keep the counter's apron state on set.
- **239:** no weapons and no muzzle flash anywhere in frame — the moderation-safe composition: the wrist at the sleeve, the open rear door, and twenty metres of wet asphalt do the work. The gunfire that follows plays off-frame and is never boarded.
- **240:** Mara's death is staged as care: no blood on the floor, no gore — her side only darkens under Jack's hands, and the frame's subject is the lights coming on.
- **236:** no people by design; the trap is still asleep.

## Batch five — Vera's hair at the bar (26 September 2026)

The director caught the one hair drift in the film: shot 235's Vera had been generated dark-haired with a fringe, against `sheets/vera.jpg`'s ash-blonde, no-fringe, pale-blue-eyed American (the drift came from a prompt written from memory, and my clock retake repeated it). An audit montage of every Vera frame (4, 52, 56, 58, 65, 72, 73, 76, 79, 97) confirmed 235 as the single outlier; it was retaken with the sheets and `s72/69-the-wait.jpg` attached and an explicit no-dark-hair lock, keeping the 9:58 clock, the dress, the umbrella and the pianist. The board NOTE now carries the lock, and verify asserts the substring "ash-blonde" on shot 235 so no future pass can drift it again.
