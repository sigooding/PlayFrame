# LET THE RAPTURES COMMENCE — SERIES PROMPT

*8 × 45min British black comedy*

## THE CAUSE

In 1980 an angel absconds. He doesn't want to administer creation, he wants to live inside it — so he steals a terminal, comes down, and edits his own appearance, confidence, charisma and musical ability to the ceiling. He leaves social status at default and never touches it. He conjures a guitar, writes a song by entering tempo and key, and becomes a rock star. He snaps the tracker off the machine's casing and gives it to a girl he loves as a keepsake. Five years later he dies of a heart attack in a hotel room, and when the machine asks whether he'd like to undo it, he says no.

The terminal passes through an estate into unclaimed storage. Forty-five years later **Martin**, a failed musician who has spent his life collecting that rockstar's belongings, wins the lot at auction and brings home a beige box he assumes is period junk. His fourteen-year-old son **Max** asks to borrow something authentically old for an esports team poster. Max misspells his team name — The Raptors — as **RAPTURES**, types the tagline **LET THE RAPTURES COMMENCE**, and is presented with a parameter menu he takes for a character-stat card. The fields are moral criteria: mercy, honesty, restraint, charity. He shuffles the values between fields so the bars look balanced on the poster, sets them below average because in a game you want the enemies weak, and asks the machine to clear his team's past attempts — meaning two seasons of bad placements he doesn't want on the card. Then he minimises the window, which sends the signal.

Four billion people are sorted by a child's layout decision. The criteria are decoupled from their values, so the sorting is not corrupt, it is arbitrary — and nobody on earth or in the afterlife can find the pattern, because there isn't one. Nobody is logged in above the dead angel to stop any of it.

## TONE

Black comedy. Dry, deadpan, English. Douglas Adams and *Dirk Gently* for the bureaucracy. The police are stupid in the manner of *Dirk Gently* or *Common Side Effects* — loose dialogue, wrong details noticed at length, long pauses, no punchlines.

**This is not noir and not a tragedy.** The visual styles differ by thread; the register never does. Every thread is the same comedy, shot differently. No pathos beats, no quiet melancholy inserts, no reflective endings, no grief. The grim material is texture.

Nobody in the series knows they're in an apocalypse — they know they're in a *situation*, and each character has translated the end of the world into the terms they already used. The cops got a promotion. Hell has a reporting problem. Grandma was vindicated. Martin's provenance has been ruined.

Let the funny thing be the sad thing and don't choose between them. Let the bureaucracy be sincere — the guides genuinely need the branch of Christianity for the form. Nothing dramatic ever happens dramatically: the rapture, the violence and the correction all occur with no flash, no sound and no score, and the camera keeps operating as though the subject has merely walked out of frame.

## COSMOLOGY

No God and no AI god. Separate universes for each religion, and an administration between them. Nobody ever meets God; nobody can get one on the phone. The whole thing has been running on an administrative layer left on autopilot — angels don't have power, they have permissions.

**Limbo** is a cathedral: stone, vaulting, stained glass a hundred feet up throwing coloured light on everyone, ceiling vanishing into haze. And bolted into it, the admin — rope barriers screwed into marble, a ticket dispenser cable-tied to a pillar, folding tables with laminated signs, a modern LED departures board zip-tied over something ancient. Packed with four billion irritated people of every religion, all absorbed in the queue rather than the fact of being dead, none of them looking up. Four desks open. Now serving: 41. Sorting has stalled, so nothing moves, and the guides have no records on anyone.

**Hell** is a corporate open-plan floor. Grey carpet tiles, hot desks, one strip light out, a blank target board, somebody's deflating birthday balloons. Intake has collapsed. Everyone there is thick as fuck.

**Limbo is unaffected by time.**

## THE BLANKS (NPCs)

They always existed — accountants, the ones who bore you to death, the men who do everything their wife tells them and apologise if they get it wrong. Baseline. The rapture didn't damage them; a few started glitching afterwards, and Heaven and Hell now use them as cameras, because an unrendered person is an unguarded window.

Their tell is **"I can only apologise"** — a complete response with nothing behind it. No reason, no excuse, no defensiveness, nothing to argue with. Half of real humanity says it too, which makes every test for them worthless, and the cops will build a screening method out of it and never doubt it. Limbo has no records on them. Nobody can be told apart from a merely tedious man. Nicknames vary by faction: extras, blanks, monos, greycoats.

## SEASON CLOCK: THE WATER

Nobody's left who knows how to run a pump, and nobody is dosing the reservoirs. Fine in ep1 (one ignored complaint about pressure), brown by ep3, upstairs taps dead by ep5, dry by ep6, people dying of it dully by ep7. The apocalypse is a maintenance backlog. The only clean water left is sitting in houses, which is what makes the burglaries inevitable and what the cops are enforcing property law over.

## CAST

**Sister Nina Doyle, 45.** Runs St Jude's, a residential house for people the system gave up on. Stubborn, self-sufficient, won't accept help, lies fluently to authority, can re-plumb a building. Says a thing once and acts as though it's settled. Gentle with the genuinely broken, brutal with anyone coasting. Her faith is functional — God is her employer and she has views about the management. Daughter of the 1980 rockstar; never knew him, and assumed her mother's story was a fantasy. Half-angel, therefore ineligible for sorting, which she'd find annoying rather than tragic — forty-five years of vocation and it turns out to be nepotism. Treats divine instruction as an unreliable supplier sending vague orders, and complains upward. Other people start calling her a saint. She finds this ridiculous and can't get rid of them.

**PC Kath Bridges (white, 30s)** and **PC Ray Okonjo (black, 30s).** Sincerely believe they were left behind to teach moral justice and can't remember who told them that. Terrible officers. Never frightened, never amazed; their emotional response to the apocalypse is mild grievance. They maintain paperwork religiously and cite superiors who no longer exist. They notice the wrong things with total commitment and follow them nowhere. Each thinks the other is the one who gets carried away.

**Danny, 40, and Jodie, 11.** Scavenging for water. He has rules — nothing off anyone still alive — and she can already see the rules are going to get them killed. She's better at crime than he is and knows it, without cruelty. He can't let her be right because the rules are the last bit of parenting he's got. Her mother was raptured.

**Martin, 40s.** Failed musician. Denim jacket correct in 1994, hair long at the back on principle, four guitars, nothing written in eleven years. Spent his life hunting the rockstar's belongings at estate sales. Owns the machine and has it filed as period junk. Can't reach his son. Aggrieved rather than grieving. Never finds out what the machine is.

**Max, 14.** Flashbacks only, alive, unreachable. Not wracked with guilt over four billion people — worried he's in trouble. Trash-talks his mates while setting their values. The one character the series doesn't laugh at.

**Brenda (50s)** and **Gordon (60s).** Limbo guides. Folding table, cardigans over robes, customer-service warmth, thirty years in post. Sincere. Bored. Cannot tell the religions apart any more.

**Vassago (50s, short sleeves, lanyard, in charge, no idea), Reek (30s, no opinions), Tamsin (20s, keen, folder).** Hell's intake. They don't notice the collapse for ages because they're worried about the balloons. Tamsin proposes that Heaven has stolen their damned for the numbers; it becomes the working theory instantly, and they investigate entirely the wrong thing all season. Tamsin starts enjoying being a person.

**Pat, 70s.** A demon running Hell's audit disguised as an old lady, because nobody suspects her and she can ask the most invasive questions on earth and have it read as nosiness. Disguise overcommitted: too many doilies, a tin containing only boiled sweets, too much rouge, a husband whose name changes. She has water, which is bait. She'll keep an arrangement going after violence and be a bit frosty about it, like a neighbour after a parking dispute.

**Hell's third field officer.** Has a blank chained in a cellar and is interrogating it to find out where the damned went. All she gets is the apology, which she reads as the system refusing to break. It's just a man doing what he's told. She has notes. She thinks she's close.

**Two angels.** Sent down to recover the absconder's missing equipment. Smug rather than stupid, and equally useless.

**Alan, 50s.** A blank Nina picks up. Fleece, sensible shoes, a bag for life containing another bag.

## VISUAL GRAMMAR — one per thread, never mixed

- **Nina** — locked off, wide, deep focus, daylight, symmetrical. The camera never follows her; she walks into frames and out of them. Long lenses on the road. The only handheld in her thread is the vision.
- **Cops** — static two-shot through the windscreen from the bonnet, plus a wide from across the car park. Sitcom frame, boring, symmetrical. Handheld only when they commit violence, then straight back to the identical framing as if nothing happened. No reaction cuts.
- **Danny & Jodie** — handheld, tight, dark. Red practical sources only: a bike light, a torch with a red filter, a standby LED. Never a wide establishing shot; we never see a whole room.
- **Limbo** — frontal, proscenium, Python staging. Vertical compositions with people tiny at the base, very low angles looking up into nothing, then hard cuts to the desk, flat and close. Every word at the desk reverberates four seconds; the guides talk over it, nobody else has learned to. Crowded.
- **Hell / demons on earth** — surveillance. Fixed high-corner cameras, 4:3, faint burnt-in timecode, slight fisheye. Cut between fixed angles only. Never moves, never close.
- **Martin** — fluorescent, flat, unlovely. Locked off, framings slightly off-centre. Institutional beige.
- **1980** — warm, grainy, anamorphic, heavy practical sources through net curtains. Sodium and tungsten. Deliberately beautiful.
- **The visions** — total break in style. Wrong aspect ratio, dropped frames, blown out, a hiss. Badly exposed home video, not white light and choral swell. She's receiving the tracker's recorded view, not God's plan.
- **Episode eight, the arcade** — all grammars collapse. Neutral, ordinary, unstyled. The only location in the series shot in no style at all.

## CONNECTIVE OBJECTS

Things travel between threads; characters mostly don't. The **knife** from the cold open, in the old woman's handbag, into the support group, and eventually to Jodie. The **pendant**, which is Nina's bearing and everyone else's tracker — every use lights her up for Heaven and Hell, so every clue costs her a head start. **Max's minimised window**, whose signal draws all four factions to the same postcode. The **boring blank** Danny and Jodie rescue, which Hell is watching through — so the kindest act in the series puts the group on Hell's map.

**Nobody ever explains the whole thing to anyone.** No catch-up scenes. Each character ends the season understanding about 30% of what happened. The audience is the only one holding all of it.

## EPISODES

**1 — no mechanism revealed.** The mugging. Nina at St Jude's and the rapture. The cops: birds, arrest ourselves, the taser. Nina's aftermath, the pendant, the first vision, she drives. Martin at the storage unit. Tag: 1980, ending on `NO`.

**2 — there's an afterlife and it's badly run.** Limbo: the family, the branches of Christianity, the seventy-two virgins, the husband led away. Danny and Jodie's first raid. Hell's intake floor. Nina's first wrong lockup; she acquires Alan.

**3 — blanks, and both sides come down.** Demons and angels arrive, badly. The blanks established. The cops invent a worthless test. Danny and Jodie recruited by the underground group. Brown water. Martin comes home to a collection and no son.

**4 — the old lady.** Jodie on the settee, Danny in the kitchen, the knife and fork, the fork through the forearm, the bottles by the gate. Survivors' meetings begin; grandma and the dog is reported as proof they're here to maintain order. Someone mentions a nun.

**5 — the basement.** The chained blank, the interrogation that goes nowhere, the rescue of a man who talks about laminate flooring. The cops pick up Danny's scent. Max flashback: the misspelling.

**6 — dry.** The taps fail. The group turns on itself; a moment of clarity. Nina gets a bearing that isn't wrong and is found by people who've decided she's a saint. The cops arrest a demon and the paperwork won't stick. Max flashback: the stat bars.

**7 — convergence.** Four parties, four wrong reasons, one postcode. Martin already there with the machine plugged in, trying to make it play something. Limbo: someone in administration realises nobody is auditing them either. Max flashback: "just clear our past attempts."

**8 — the arcade.** Everyone in one building, all wrong about why. Martin defends his collection from people trying to save the world. The cops arrest people in sequence. Demons and angels arrive simultaneously and each assumes the other is what they came for. Upstairs: Jodie gets through because she's eleven and ignored; Nina can't read the menu; Max knows where the fields are. The reversal won't authorise — Limbo administration is protecting the outage, because a stalled queue means nobody audits them. Nina wins an argument at a desk. Then the correction: not a rewind, but a bid on an auction lot, placed by a nun, outbidding Martin for the box the machine is in. `ENTER`. No flash, no sound.

**Last shots.** The storage unit, shutter down, the machine unopened in the dark. The lot sticker: a number, a date, SOLD. Then Limbo, untouched by time, the queue exactly as it was, and a man at the desk saying he's just got one question. No epilogue, no faces, nothing resolved for anyone.

## OUTPUT FORMAT

Numbered shots. Each shot: shot type, lens, how the camera is mounted, then a short plain description. Dialogue indented beneath the shot it falls in. Mark pauses in seconds where the pause is the joke. No scene-setting prose, no camera poetry, no explanation of subtext.
