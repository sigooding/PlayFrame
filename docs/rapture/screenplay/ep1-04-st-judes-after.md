# ST JUDE'S - AFTER

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - DINING ROOM - LATER

Half the plates are cleared. Nina works down the table with a bin bag, scraping full English breakfasts into it. Methodical. Ten to go.

She reaches Terry's place. Looks at the seat. Looks at the plate.

Scrapes it.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - KITCHEN - LATER

She washes up twenty covers. Through the window behind her, an ordinary street where nothing is happening at all.

The tap coughs. Brown for a second. Clears. She turns it off and on again. Fine. She carries on.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - HALL - LATER

The dog is sitting in the middle of the corridor, looking at her.

NINA
I know.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - UTILITY ROOM - LATER

She turns the gas off at the meter. Checks it. Turns it again to be sure.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - DORMITORY - LATER

Six beds. She strips the first, folds the sheet properly, corners in. Moves to the second.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - COL'S ROOM - LATER

She opens a drawer. Inside, wrapped in a jumper: Deborah's television aerial.

NINA
(almost fond)
You little sod.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - MAUREEN'S ROOM - LATER

She lifts the pillow. Underneath: two jars of the good coffee from the staff cupboard. One for the taking, one for the inconvenience.

She looks at them for a moment.

She does not put them back. She takes both.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - OFFICE - LATER

She picks up the landline. Dial tone. Dials three digits. Listens. Nothing.

Puts it down. Picks it up. Same. Puts it down.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - HALL - LATER

She pins a note to the noticeboard and squares it with the edge.

INSERT - THE NOTE

GONE OUT. DO NOT TOUCH THE BOILER. — N.

EXT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - YARD - LATER

An old minibus, bonnet up. Nina underneath it, half out of view, working on the fuel line. Competent. Filthy.

She drops the bonnet, wipes her hands on a rag, gets in. Turns the key. Nothing. Turns it again. It catches.

No reaction whatsoever.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - KITCHEN - LATER

She fills four water containers at the tap. The flow is weak and discoloured. She frowns at it.

NINA
(to the tap)
Don't you start.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - NINA'S ROOM - LATER

A narrow single bed, made. A wooden cross on the wall, slightly crooked. She opens her bedside drawer. Elastic bands, a rosary with a broken link, a nail file, and a pendant on a cheap chain. Dull. Unremarkable. Her mother's.

She picks it up because it's in the way of the torch batteries. Looks at it the way you look at something you've owned for forty years and never worn.

She puts it on anyway. It's smaller round her neck than in a bag.

INT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - CORRIDOR - LATER

A long hallway. Doors open. Sunlight in bars across the floor. Nina walks the length of it with a bag over her shoulder, the dog at her heel.

Halfway along she stops. Her hand goes to her chest.

Not awe. Discomfort, then irritation. She pulls the chain out of her collar. The pendant shifts very slightly in her palm, like something settling.

FLASHES — WRONG ASPECT RATIO, DROPPED FRAMES, BLOWN OUT, A HISS:

A beige computer. A green cursor.

A man's hand on a keyboard. Not hers.

A hotel room. Net curtains. The wrong decade.

A roller shutter. A painted bay number. Strip lights.

BACK TO SCENE

Nina in the corridor. Breathing hard. Then, immediately, composing herself, like someone who's had a dizzy spell and won't be fussed over.

NINA
(to the ceiling, flatly)
That's not an address.

She tucks the pendant away. Looks back down a corridor in a house she has finished tidying. One nod. Job done.

EXT. ST JUDE'S HOUSE - YARD - LATER

She loads the bus. Water first. The dog gets in without being invited.

She locks the front door. Tries the handle. Tries it again.

The bus pulls out and away down the lane. The house sits behind it, sunlit, full of nothing.

EXT. OPEN ROAD - DAY

The minibus, tiny, on a long straight empty road. Heat shimmer.
