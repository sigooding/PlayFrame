# LET THE RAPTURES COMMENCE
## EPISODE FOUR — SCENE 2

Scene: INT./EXT. PAT'S HOUSE — DUSK INTO NIGHT
Cast: PAT (70s), JODIE (11), DANNY (40), MALCOLM (unseen, below)
Grammar: two grammars, never blended within a shot. Front room: lamplit, warm, flat, frontal, static, tidy — hers. Kitchen and garden: handheld, close, dark, red torchlight — Danny and Jodie's. The cut between rooms should feel like a cut between two different programmes. Shots 31–32 switch to the demon surveillance grammar (fixed high corner, 4:3, burnt-in timecode); shots 33–35 snap back to the Crane grammar, abruptly.

The point: Scene 2 plays with the audience already knowing there is a man under the floor, because the cold open planted him. Pat audits Jodie like a questionnaire and gets better data than Hell's intake ever did. The violence is silent and domestic — the exact rhythm of someone eating a meal — and afterwards her grammar simply returns, mid-sentence.

---

1. INT. FRONT ROOM — STATIC WIDE, 32mm, frontal, dead centre
Immaculate. Two table lamps on, curtains not yet drawn, the window going blue behind. Doilies on everything, including things that cannot take a doily. Jodie on the settee with a glass of squash. Pat opposite, delighted.
   PAT: And your mum?
   JODIE: Gone.
   PAT: Gone where?
   JODIE: Up.
   PAT: (warmly) Isn't that nice.

2. INSERT — 85mm, static, lamplight
The sweet tin, open, offered. Only boiled sweets. Levelled flat.

3. STATIC MEDIUM — 50mm, frontal, on JODIE
She takes one. She's eleven. Free sweets are free sweets.

4. INT. KITCHEN — HANDHELD, 28mm, close, red torchlight
Unlit. Danny going through cupboards by torch, silent. He opens one: slabs of bottled water, floor to shelf. He cannot believe his luck.

5. INT. FRONT ROOM — STATIC TWO-SHOT, 40mm, frontal
   PAT: And your dad. Is he a good man?
   JODIE: He's all right.
   PAT: Does he take things?
   JODIE: (a beat too long) No.
   PAT: Never?
   JODIE: Not off anyone who's still alive. He won't.
   PAT: (genuinely interested for the first time) Won't he.

6. CLOSE — 65mm, static, frontal, on PAT
Lamplight on too much rouge. Fascinated. Not by the child. By the data.
   PAT: And where do you sleep, love?
   JODIE: The old scout hut. Off the bypass.
   PAT: How many of you?
   JODIE: Twenty-something.

7. INSERT — 85mm, static
A framed wedding photograph on the sideboard. The man's face is slightly wrong, like it was added later.
   JODIE: Is that your husband?
   PAT: That's my Derek.
   JODIE: You said Malcolm.
   PAT: (no pause whatsoever) Malcolm's the other one.

8. STATIC MEDIUM — 50mm, frontal, on JODIE
She accepts this. She's eleven and old people are confusing.

9. INT. KITCHEN — HANDHELD, 35mm, red
Bottles into a bag. Too many. The handles strain. The torch clamped under his chin.

10. INT. KITCHEN — HANDHELD, 28mm
He turns and knocks a mug off the draining board. It hits the lino. It doesn't break. In a silent house it is enormous.

11. INT. FRONT ROOM — STATIC TWO-SHOT, 40mm, frontal
Pat stops. Mid-word.

Nothing on her face changes. She stands up and walks out of the room.

She does not excuse herself.

12. STATIC MEDIUM — 50mm, frontal, on JODIE
Alone in a lamplit room with a glass of squash, looking at an empty chair.

13. INT. KITCHEN — HANDHELD, 35mm
Danny frozen, bag in hand, torch beam on the floor. The hall light comes on behind Pat as she enters — she's backlit, shape only.
   PAT: (warm, apologetic) Oh — you gave me a fright.

14. HANDHELD CLOSE — 50mm, on DANNY
He starts an explanation. He has nothing.
   DANNY: I — the door was —

15. HANDHELD CLOSE — 65mm, on PAT
His torch comes up onto her face. Still the old lady. Cardigan, rouge, concerned expression.

Then the mouth opens. Too wide. Far too wide, jaw unhinged past anything a face should do. The eyes go black, cornea to cornea.

Everything else about her is unchanged. She is still standing politely in a cardigan in her own kitchen.

16. HANDHELD — 28mm, wide, chaotic, torch swinging
She crosses the kitchen in two steps. Faster than a person. Much faster than a pensioner.

17. INSERT — 85mm, handheld, red
Her hands in the torchlight. A knife in one, a fork in the other, out of her own drawer, held the way you hold cutlery at a table.

18. HANDHELD — 35mm, violent, close, the torch on the floor now and only lighting the ceiling
She's on him. Both arms going up and down, alternating, knife and fork, over and over — the exact rhythm of someone eating a meal.

Silent. No shriek, no roar. Nothing.

19. HANDHELD — 28mm, the window over the sink
Danny gets it open and goes through, half falling, half throwing himself.

20. INSERT — 85mm, handheld
The fork goes through his forearm on the way out. It stays in for a second. It comes out with him.

21. EXT. BACK GARDEN — HANDHELD, 35mm, near dark
He lands in a flowerbed. Gets up. The bag of water is still in his other hand. He never let go of it.

22. EXT. GARDEN — HANDHELD WIDE, 28mm, from the fence
Over the fence, badly. Blood down his sleeve, black in this light.

23. INT. KITCHEN — STATIC MEDIUM, 50mm, frontal — her grammar returns
Pat at the open window, lit from the hall behind her. Face entirely normal. Cardigan straight. She calls out into the dark in the voice she was using in the front room.
   PAT: I'll leave the bottles by the gate, love.

24. INSERT — 85mm, static
She puts the knife and fork in the sink. Runs the tap over them. The water comes out brown. She lets it run anyway.

25. INT. FRONT ROOM — STATIC TWO-SHOT, 40mm, frontal
Pat comes back in, sits down, picks up her tea, and resumes exactly where she left off.
   PAT: — because Derek never liked the bypass.

26. STATIC MEDIUM — 50mm, frontal, on JODIE
Eleven years old. Glass of squash. She heard the window. She heard the line about the bottles. She has worked out precisely what is happening and precisely what happens if she gets it wrong.
   JODIE: (evenly) No.

27. STATIC TWO-SHOT — 40mm, frontal
   PAT: Another sweet?
   JODIE: No thank you.
   PAT: Go on.
   JODIE: (taking one) Thank you.

28. STATIC WIDE — 32mm, frontal, dead centre — same framing as shot 1
Jodie stands. Puts her glass on a doily. Pat watches her do it. The window behind them is dark now.
   JODIE: I'd better go.
   PAT: It's getting dark, love.
   JODIE: I know.
   PAT: Will you come again?
   JODIE: (a beat) Probably.
   PAT: (delighted) Good.

29. STATIC WIDE — 28mm, frontal, the hall
Jodie walks to the front door. It's a long hall, lit by one bulb. Pat doesn't get up.
Jodie passes the door under the stairs. She doesn't look at it.
She lets herself out.

30. EXT. FRONT PATH — STATIC WIDE, 35mm — still her grammar, not theirs
Jodie walks down the path at a completely normal pace, out of the light from the hall and into the dark. Through the gate. Doesn't run. Doesn't look back.

31. INT. HALL — FIXED CAM, high corner, 4:3, timecode — demon grammar
The cellar door opens. Malcolm comes up. Turns the key behind him.

32. FIXED CAM — the kitchen, high corner
He stops. The window is open to the dark. A mug on the lino. A fork in the sink.
He looks at the water cupboard. The door is ajar.
   MALCOLM: (o.s.) Patricia.
   PAT: (o.s., from the front room, entirely pleasant) Mm.
   MALCOLM: Where's the water gone?

(Pause.)
   PAT: (o.s.) I've made a friend.

33. EXT. PAVEMENT — HANDHELD, 35mm — their grammar, abruptly
Jodie rounds the corner. Danny's against a wall, white, holding his arm, bag at his feet.
   DANNY: We're not coming back here.
   JODIE: (looking at the bag) How much did you get?
   DANNY: Jodie —
   JODIE: She's leaving the rest by the gate.

34. HANDHELD CLOSE — 50mm, on DANNY
A man who is bleeding and has just understood he is going to come back.

35. HANDHELD WIDE — 28mm
The two of them walking away into the dark. She's carrying the bag. He isn't.
Hold.
