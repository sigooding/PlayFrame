# LET THE RAPTURES COMMENCE
## EPISODE FOUR — SCENE 5

Scene: THE DOORSTEP — EXT./INT. MARTIN'S HOUSE — DAY
Cast: NINA (45), MARTIN (40s), ALAN (in the bus)
Grammar: her grammar on the street and in the hall — locked off, wide, deep focus, symmetrical, daylight. His grammar in the house — fluorescent, flat, framings slightly off-centre. They should sit badly together, and the film does not resolve it: two threads in one building before they are one thread. Nothing in either grammar moves.

She is still doing the audit from the estate. Eleven houses this morning, going in, looking at a room, coming out. The twelfth opens a door on a man who has not spoken to anybody in eight weeks, and the bedroom she was sent to is the one with the machine in it — which she does not clock at all, and neither does the camera.

---

1. STATIC WIDE — EXT. CUL-DE-SAC — 28mm, locked off, symmetrical
An ordinary road of semis in flat daylight. The minibus parked in the middle of it. Nina at a front door. She knocks. Waits. Nothing. Moves to the next house.

2. STATIC WIDE — EXT. CUL-DE-SAC — 28mm, locked off, identical framing
Next door. Knocks. Waits. Nothing. Moves on.

3. STATIC WIDE — EXT. CUL-DE-SAC — 28mm, locked off, identical framing again
Next door. Knocks. Waits.
The door opens.

4. STATIC MEDIUM — EXT. FRONT PATH — 50mm, locked off, frontal, from the path
MARTIN in the doorway. Denim jacket. He hasn't spoken to anybody in eight weeks and it shows.
   MARTIN: (genuinely thrown) Hello.
   NINA: Afternoon.
   MARTIN: You knocked.
   NINA: I did.
   MARTIN: (a beat) Nobody's knocked.

5. STATIC MEDIUM — EXT. FRONT PATH — 50mm, locked off, reverse, on NINA
Bag over her shoulder. Forty-five. Entirely businesslike.
   NINA: I'm looking for a bedroom.
(Pause.)
   MARTIN: Right.
   NINA: A child's.
   MARTIN: (pause) Right.
   NINA: I'm a nun.
   MARTIN: (a longer pause) Is that meant to help?

6. STATIC WIDE — EXT. FRONT PATH — 24mm, locked off, the doorstep
Neither of them moves. Behind her, the bus. Alan is visible through the windscreen, upright, facing forward.
   NINA: I've done eleven houses this morning. I go in, I look at a room, I come out. Nobody's been in yet.
   MARTIN: In where?
   NINA: (flatly) Alive.

7. CLOSE — EXT. FRONT PATH — 65mm, static, on MARTIN
A man weighing up whether this is the worst thing to happen to him today or the best.
   MARTIN: Have you got a warrant?
   NINA: I've got a bus.

8. STATIC WIDE — EXT. FRONT PATH — 24mm, locked off
He stands aside.

9. STATIC WIDE — INT. HALL — 24mm, locked off, his grammar now, flat and off-centre
She comes in. Stops.

10. STATIC WIDE — INT. LIVING ROOM — 28mm, locked off
A room that is 70% collection. Framed things. Records in sleeves. Two guitars on stands. Nowhere comfortable to sit.

11. CLOSE — INT. LIVING ROOM — 65mm, static, on NINA
She takes it in. She does not comment. Twenty years of front rooms.
   MARTIN: (behind her, defensive already) It's a collection.
   NINA: Mm.
   MARTIN: It's not — I'm not one of these that just —
   NINA: Where's the bedroom?

12. STATIC WIDE — INT. FOOT OF THE STAIRS — 24mm, locked off
   MARTIN: Whose?
   NINA: Have you got a child?
   MARTIN: (carefully) Why?
   NINA: Because I'd like to see his room.
(A long pause. From Martin's side of this, it is an appalling request.)
   MARTIN: I'm coming up with you.
   NINA: I'd think less of you if you didn't.

13. STATIC WIDE — INT. LANDING — 28mm, locked off
Two people on a landing. He opens a door.

14. STATIC MEDIUM — INT. BEDROOM DOORWAY — 50mm, locked off
A fourteen-year-old's room. Unmade bed. Curtains half shut. A monitor, dark.
A poster on the wall: THE RAPTORS. Last season's. Correctly spelled.

15. CLOSE — INT. BEDROOM — 65mm, static, on NINA
She looks at the poster. She's seen those letters. She doesn't know what they are.

16. STATIC WIDE — INT. BEDROOM — 24mm, locked off
She steps in. Martin stays in the doorway with his arms folded.

17. STATIC MEDIUM — INT. BEDROOM — 50mm, locked off
She looks at the bed. The curtains. The walls. Working, not moved.

18. INSERT — INT. BEDROOM — 85mm, static
The desk. And on it, plugged in, dusty: a beige unbranded terminal and a CRT.

19. CLOSE — INT. BEDROOM — 65mm, static, on NINA
Nothing. She's looked at eleven bedrooms this morning and this is another one.
She glances at the machine the way you'd glance at a printer.

20. STATIC MEDIUM — INT. BEDROOM — 50mm, locked off
She moves past it toward the window.
And stops.

21. CLOSE — INT. BEDROOM — 65mm, static
Her hand goes to her chest.

22. INSERT — INT. BEDROOM — 100mm macro, static
The pendant, pulled out of her collar. In her palm.
It lights. Not a glow — a hard, small, mechanical light, like something coming back on after forty-five years.

23. CLOSE — INT. BEDROOM — 65mm, static, on NINA
Not awe. Not wonder.
Vindication, and then — immediately — annoyance.
   NINA: (under her breath) Eleven houses.

24. STATIC WIDE — INT. BEDROOM — 28mm, locked off
She turns and looks at the beige box on the desk.

25. STATIC MEDIUM — INT. BEDROOM DOORWAY — 50mm, locked off
Martin, arms folded, watching a woman he let in four minutes ago react to a computer.
   MARTIN: What's it doing?
   NINA: (not looking at him) Whose is that?
   MARTIN: Mine.
   NINA: Where'd you get it?
   MARTIN: Auction.
   NINA: What is it?
   MARTIN: (a shrug) Nothing. It's a job lot. There's no badge on it.

26. INSERT — INT. BEDROOM — 85mm, static
The casing. And on the top, a small rectangular gap in the plastic where something has been snapped off.

27. CLOSE — INT. BEDROOM — 65mm, static, on NINA
She looks at the gap. She looks at the thing in her hand.
She says nothing at all.

28. STATIC WIDE — INT. BEDROOM — 24mm, locked off
Two people and a computer.
   MARTIN: Are you going to tell me what's going on?
   NINA: No.
   MARTIN: Right.
   NINA: (a beat, reconsidering) Have you got a kettle?
   MARTIN: Water's brown.
   NINA: I've got water.

29. STATIC MEDIUM — INT. BEDROOM DOORWAY — 50mm, locked off
He looks at her. Then at the machine. Then at his son's empty bed.
   MARTIN: He borrowed it. For a poster.

30. CLOSE — INT. BEDROOM — 65mm, static, on NINA
The first thing anyone has said to her in eight weeks that means something.
   NINA: Did he.

31. STATIC WIDE — INT. LANDING — 28mm, locked off
She comes out. He shuts the bedroom door behind them. She stops him.
   NINA: Leave it open.
   MARTIN: Why?
   NINA: (already going downstairs) Because I'm coming back up.

32. STATIC WIDE — EXT. CUL-DE-SAC — 28mm, locked off, her grammar, symmetrical, from the road
The house. The bus. Alan still facing forward in the passenger seat, hasn't moved, hasn't noticed anything.
Hold.
